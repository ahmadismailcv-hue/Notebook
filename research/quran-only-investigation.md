## 1. Executive overview

**The core finding.** The Qur'an is precise in some areas and procedurally thin in others, and the two are not distributed at random:

- **Detailed:** inheritance shares, debt documentation, divorce waiting periods, oath expiation, ablution, food prohibitions, penalties for specific offences, substitutions when the normal act is impossible.
- **Thin:** the bodily and verbal form of prayer, the number of daily prayers, the ritual sequence of Hajj, zakat rates, the precise limits of the fast.

**The pattern behind it** (developed in §13). The Qur'an mostly legislates *exceptions, substitutions and disputes* around ritual norms that it does not itself describe. It tells you what to do if you have no water, if you are travelling, if you are afraid, if you are ill, if you are prevented from completing the pilgrimage. It does not describe the baseline act these provisions modify. The text presupposes that its audience already knew what *ṣalāh*, *ḥajj*, *zakāh* and *ṣiyām* were. It says so directly in several places: prayer existed among earlier prophets and even among the pagans of Mecca (8:35), fasting was "prescribed for those before you" (2:183), and the Hajj months are "well known" (2:197).

**What follows from this:**

1. **High-confidence obligations** can be established from the text alone: monotheistic worship, regular prayer at specified parts of the day with ablution and a direction, a month of daytime fasting in Ramadan, giving of wealth with defined recipient categories, pilgrimage to the House for those able, detailed family and inheritance rules, food prohibitions, abstention from intoxicants and gambling, and a dense ethical code.
2. **Ritual form** (how many prayers, how many units, which words, what sequence, how many circuits) cannot be fixed from the text. A Qur'an-only community would have to choose. Different communities would choose differently, and the text gives them no means to say that one is wrong.
3. **The Qur'an does not settle the authority question decisively in either direction.** Some verses describe the Messenger's duty as only "clear delivery" and present the Book as "detailed" and "sufficient". Other verses show that God gave the Prophet information and institutions that are not in the text: an earlier prayer direction (2:143), a private disclosure (66:3), a promise before battle (8:7), and a permission (59:5). They also command obedience to his judgements during disputes (4:65). Neither side has a knockdown text. The decisive variable is **an assumption the Qur'an does not resolve**: whether obedience addressed to people in the Prophet's presence carries forward to material that is not in the Qur'an.
4. **About Muhammad**, the Qur'an supports a thin but real portrait. He was a named orphan who was raised to sufficiency. He was not a reader of earlier scriptures. He received revelation, was opposed and expelled, fought named and unnamed battles, and had several wives and at least some daughters. He was rebuked on specific occasions. He was mortal and fallible in conduct while preserved in delivery. A *biography* in the narrative sense cannot be reconstructed: there are no dates, no age, no parents' names and no account of his death.

**A challenge to the premise itself.** "Qur'an alone" is not a fully self-contained position, for two reasons:

1. **The text needs a reading tradition.** The Qur'an cannot establish which recited text, verse division and vocalisation constitute "the Qur'an". It claims to be preserved (15:9) but does not identify a manuscript.
2. **Its words need Arabic outside the Qur'an.** Many crucial words occur only once or twice (*qurū'*, *tafath*, *ṣalāt al-wusṭā*). Their meaning depends on Arabic usage outside the Qur'an.

So the real choice is never "Qur'an vs. external sources". It is **which external sources, and how much weight each gets**. This report keeps external input to the minimum needed to read Arabic. It flags each place where a lexical or textual decision does work that the Qur'an itself does not do.

## 2. Methodology and textual limitations

### 2.1 Rules applied

- The text quoted is the Hafs recitation with Cairo numbering. Where the argument turns on a single vowel (e.g. 5:6, *arjulakum*), I note it.
- Claims are graded using the requested five-level scale:
  - **Explicit**
  - **Strongly inferable**
  - **Ambiguous**
  - **Unspecified**
  - **Potentially contradictory**
- Arabic semantics are argued from the Qur'an's own usage wherever possible: how the same root behaves in other verses. This is the only principled substitute for outside lexicons.
- Contemporary Muslim practice is mentioned **only as a comparison target**: "can this element be established?". It is never used as evidence.

### 2.2 Structural limits of any Qur'an-only method

| Limit | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Text identity** | The Qur'an does not specify its own canonical reading, verse divisions, or order. 75:17 (*inna ʿalaynā jamʿahu wa-qurʾānah*, "upon Us is its collection and its recitation") is a claim about divine responsibility, not a specification. |
| **Lexical dependence** | Hapax or near-hapax terms (*tafath* 22:29, *qurū'* 2:228, *kalāla* 4:12/4:176) cannot be fully defined from internal usage. |
| **Presupposed referents** | The text names practices it does not define (*ṣalāh*, *ḥajj*, *ʿumrah*, *nidāʾ* "call", *al-ashhur al-maʿlūmāt*). An internal method can show *that* a referent is presupposed. It cannot show *what* it was. |
| **Deontic ambiguity** | Imperatives are not always obligations. In 5:2 *fa-ṣṭādū* ("then hunt") and in 62:10 *fa-ntashirū* ("then disperse") are plainly permissions. So the grammatical mood alone does not settle whether something is obligatory. |
| **Addressee scope** | Many commands are in the 2nd person singular to the Prophet (e.g. 17:78, 11:114). Whether they bind the community is a matter of inference. |

### 2.3 What "inference" means here

An inference is counted as **strong** only if at least two independent passages converge on it, and no passage cuts against it. A single-verse reading is classified as **ambiguous** whenever the Arabic permits another construal.

## 3. Prayer (ṣalāh)

### 3.1 What *ṣalāh* refers to in the Qur'an

The root ṣ-l-w covers a wider range than ritual prayer:

- **Blessing / supporting prayer.** 33:56: *inna Allāha wa-malāʾikatahu yuṣallūna ʿalā al-nabī*, "God and His angels bless the Prophet". 9:103: *wa-ṣalli ʿalayhim inna ṣalātaka sakanun lahum*, "pray for them; your prayer is a comfort to them".
- **A religious practice in general.** 11:87: Shuʿayb's people ask *a-ṣalātuka taʾmuruka*, "does your prayer command you…".
- **A physical act at specific times, with preconditions.** Times: 17:78, 11:114, 24:58. Ablution: 5:6. Direction: 2:144. Bowing and prostration: 4:102, 22:77.

So the Qur'an uses *ṣalāh* both generically and as a technical rite. The ritual sense is **strongly inferable**. It is the only sense compatible with ablution, timings, direction, and "when they have prostrated" (4:102). Readings that reduce all *ṣalāh* to "connection" or "commitment" have to explain those verses away, and the text does not support that.

**Command vocabulary.** Prayer is usually commanded with *aqīmū al-ṣalāh*, "establish / keep upright the prayer" (2:43, 2:110, 4:103, 22:78, 24:56, 73:20, and many more). It is rarely commanded with *ṣallū*. The verb *ṣallā* describes performance: 4:102 *lam yuṣallū fa-l-yuṣallū maʿak*, 87:15 *fa-ṣallā*, 96:10 *ʿabdan idhā ṣallā*. See §13 for whether this distinction is meaningful.

### 3.2 Pre-existence of prayer

The Qur'an treats *ṣalāh* as already known:

- Abraham: 14:37, 14:40 (*rabbi jʿalnī muqīma al-ṣalāh*). Abraham's line: 21:73.
- Ishmael: 19:55. Moses: 20:14 (*wa-aqimi al-ṣalāta li-dhikrī*). Jesus: 19:31. Zechariah: 3:39. Mary: 3:43.
- The Children of Israel: 2:83, 5:12, 10:87 (*wa-jʿalū buyūtakum qiblatan wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh*).
- The Meccan polytheists: 8:35: *wa-mā kāna ṣalātuhum ʿinda al-bayti illā mukāʾan wa-taṣdiyah*, "their prayer at the House was nothing but whistling and clapping".

**Classification:** explicit that prayer pre-dated Muhammad's message. **Strongly inferable** that the Qur'an's audience already knew a form of the rite. The commands assume a referent and never define one.

### 3.3 Number and times of daily prayer

All the time-indicating passages:

| Verse | Arabic (key words) | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| 11:114 | وَأَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ طَرَفَيِ النَّهَارِ وَزُلَفًا مِّنَ اللَّيْلِ | Prayer at "the two ends of the day" (dual) and "near portions of the night" (*zulafan*, plural) |
| 17:78 | أَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ لِدُلُوكِ الشَّمْسِ إِلَىٰ غَسَقِ اللَّيْلِ وَقُرْآنَ الْفَجْرِ | From the sun's *dulūk* (decline/setting) to the *ghasaq* (deepening dark) of night, **and** the dawn recitation |
| 17:79 | وَمِنَ اللَّيْلِ فَتَهَجَّدْ بِهِ نَافِلَةً لَّكَ | Night vigil, explicitly *nāfilah* (supererogatory), "for you" (singular) |
| 24:58 | مِّن قَبْلِ صَلَاةِ الْفَجْرِ … وَمِن بَعْدِ صَلَاةِ الْعِشَاءِ | Two prayers **named**: *ṣalāt al-fajr* and *ṣalāt al-ʿishāʾ* |
| 2:238 | حَافِظُوا عَلَى الصَّلَوَاتِ وَالصَّلَاةِ الْوُسْطَىٰ | Plural "prayers" plus "the middle/best prayer" |
| 4:103 | إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَابًا مَّوْقُوتًا | Prayer is a timed prescription (*mawqūt*) |
| 20:130 | وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا ۖ وَمِنْ آنَاءِ اللَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْ وَأَطْرَافَ النَّهَارِ | *Tasbīḥ* before sunrise, before sunset, in night hours, at the "ends" (plural) of the day |
| 50:39–40 | قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ الْغُرُوبِ … وَأَدْبَارَ السُّجُودِ | *Tasbīḥ* before sunrise and sunset, at night, and "after the prostrations" |
| 52:48–49 | حِينَ تَقُومُ … وَإِدْبَارَ النُّجُومِ | *Tasbīḥ* when you rise, at night, at the "retreat of the stars" |
| 30:17–18 | حِينَ تُمْسُونَ وَحِينَ تُصْبِحُونَ … وَعَشِيًّا وَحِينَ تُظْهِرُونَ | Glorification at evening, morning, late afternoon (*ʿashiyyan*), and midday (*tuẓhirūn*) |

**Competing reconstructions, all text-based:**

- **Two-prayer reading.** 24:58 names only *fajr* and *ʿishāʾ*. "The two ends of the day" (11:114) can be read as dawn and dusk.
  - *Weakness:* it ignores *zulafan min al-layl* as an additional time. It also ignores *dulūk… ilā ghasaq* (17:78) as a span that could contain more than one prayer.
- **Three-prayer reading.**
  - 11:114 gives dawn, dusk, and night.
  - 17:78 gives a span from *dulūk* to *ghasaq* plus *fajr*.
  - 24:58 names two of the three.
  - *Weakness:* 2:238 *al-ṣalāh al-wusṭā* implies a "middle" prayer, which fits 3 (or 5) but not 2. However, *wusṭā* may mean "best/most balanced". Compare 2:143 *ummatan wasaṭan* and 68:28 *qāla awsaṭuhum*, "the most moderate of them". On that reading it implies no count.
- **Five-prayer reading.**
  - 11:114: *ṭarafay* (2) + *zulafan* (Arabic plural; if read as ≥3, the total is 5).
  - 30:17–18: four or five glorification times, including *tuẓhirūn* (midday) and *ʿashiyyan*.
  - 17:78: *dulūk* read as the sun's decline past noon, so the span covers noon through nightfall.
  - *Weaknesses:*
    - *zulaf* may simply mean "parts".
    - The plural-≥3 argument relies on a grammatical convention that the Qur'an does not uniformly observe.
    - 30:17–18 and 20:130 command *tasbīḥ*, not *ṣalāh*. Equating them is an inference, although 50:40 *adbār al-sujūd* links glorification to prostration.

**Classification:**

- Prayer at dawn and at night: **explicit**.
- Prayer at the "ends of the day" and across the span from the sun's decline to dark: **explicit in wording, ambiguous in reference**.
- The exact number of daily obligatory prayers: **ambiguous**. Two, three and five are all textually defensible. Three has the fewest auxiliary assumptions. Five has the most supporting passages but requires equating *tasbīḥ* with *ṣalāh*.
- Exact clock boundaries: **unspecified**. The text uses observable solar markers, not durations.

### 3.4 Physical movements

Postures the Qur'an associates with worship:

- **Standing (*qiyām*).**
  - 2:238: *wa-qūmū li-llāhi qānitīn*, "stand for God devoutly".
  - 4:102: *fa-l-taqum ṭāʾifatun minhum maʿak*.
  - 22:26: *al-qāʾimīn*.
  - 3:191: *qiyāman wa-quʿūdan wa-ʿalā junūbihim*, of remembrance.
  - 62:11: *wa-tarakūka qāʾiman*.
- **Bowing (*rukūʿ*).** 2:43 *wa-rkaʿū maʿa al-rākiʿīn*; 22:77; 48:29 *rukkaʿan sujjadan*; 77:48.
- **Prostration (*sujūd*).**
  - 4:102: *fa-idhā sajadū fa-l-yakūnū min warāʾikum*, "when they have prostrated, let them go behind you".
  - 22:77. 17:107: *yakhirrūna li-l-adhqāni sujjadā*, "they fall on their chins prostrating". 25:64. 39:9. 96:19 *wa-sjud wa-qtarib*.

**Sequence.** The only verse listing all three in a ritual setting is 22:26: *li-l-ṭāʾifīna wa-l-qāʾimīna wa-l-rukkaʿi al-sujūd*, "for those who circle, those who stand, those who bow and prostrate". The order there is standing, bowing, prostrating. But 3:43 commands Mary *wa-sjudī wa-rkaʿī*, prostration **before** bowing. So the conjunction *wa* does not encode sequence in the Qur'an, and 22:26 cannot fix an order.

**Semantic range warning:**

- 2:58 / 7:161: *wa-dkhulū al-bāba sujjadan*, "enter the gate *sujjadan*". One cannot enter a gate literally face-down, so *sujjad* here means "humbly / submissively".
- 38:24: David *kharra rākiʿan*, "fell down *rākiʿan*". "Falling" while "bowing" suggests *rukūʿ* can denote full prostration or general humbling.
- 13:15, 16:49, 22:18, 55:6: stars, trees, shadows and all in the heavens perform *sujūd*.

So the words alone do not define body positions. The physical reading in prayer is supported by context: 4:102's turn-taking, and 17:107 "on their chins". The precise configuration is not stated.

**Classification:**

- Standing, bowing and prostrating are part of prayer: **strongly inferable**.
- Their sequence: **unspecified**.
- Their number per prayer: **unspecified**.
- Sitting postures: **unspecified**. *Quʿūd* appears in 3:191 and 4:103 for remembrance, not as a ritual posture.
- The word *rakʿah* as a "unit": **absent**. The Qur'an never uses it.

### 3.5 Words and recitation

- **Speech in prayer is presupposed.** 4:43: *lā taqrabū al-ṣalāta wa-antum sukārā ḥattā taʿlamū mā taqūlūn*, "do not approach prayer drunk, until you know what you are saying".
- **Voice level is regulated.** 17:110: *wa-lā tajhar bi-ṣalātika wa-lā tukhāfit bihā wa-btaghi bayna dhālika sabīlā*, "neither loud nor inaudible, but a way between".
- **Recitation of the Qur'an at dawn.** 17:78: *qurʾān al-fajr… kāna mashhūdā*. *Qurʾān* here can mean "the dawn recitation" or metonymically "the dawn prayer". Either way, recitation is involved.
- **Recitation in night prayer.**
  - 73:4 *wa-rattili al-qurʾāna tartīlā*.
  - 73:20 *fa-qraʾū mā tayassara min al-qurʾān*, "recite what is easy of the Qur'an". This is said twice in that verse, in the context of the night vigil.
- **Exalting God (*takbīr*) and remembrance.**
  - 17:111 *wa-kabbirhu takbīrā*; 74:3 *wa-rabbaka fa-kabbir*.
  - 87:15 *wa-dhakara isma rabbihi fa-ṣallā*, "he remembered his Lord's name and prayed".
  - 20:14 prayer is *li-dhikrī*, "for My remembrance".
  - 29:45 *wa-la-dhikru Allāhi akbar*.
- **The opening chapter (al-Fātiḥah).** Its first-person-plural petitions read like a liturgy (1:5 *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*). 15:87 mentions *sabʿan min al-mathānī*, "seven of the oft-repeated". No verse instructs reciting al-Fātiḥah in prayer, and no verse identifies *al-mathānī* as al-Fātiḥah. 39:23 calls the whole book *mathāniya*.

**Classification:**

- Some speech in prayer: **explicit (presupposed)**.
- Moderate voice: **explicit**.
- Recitation of the Qur'an in dawn and night prayer: **strongly inferable**.
- Mandatory recitation of al-Fātiḥah: **unspecified**.
- Any fixed formula (opening *takbīr*, words in bowing or prostration, testimony while seated, closing greeting): **unspecified**.

### 3.6 Purity

5:6 is the most procedurally detailed ritual verse in the Qur'an:

> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا قُمْتُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَاغْسِلُوا وُجُوهَكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ إِلَى الْمَرَافِقِ وَامْسَحُوا بِرُءُوسِكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُمْ إِلَى الْكَعْبَيْنِ ۚ وَإِن كُنتُمْ جُنُبًا فَاطَّهَّرُوا ۚ وَإِن كُنتُم مَّرْضَىٰ أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ أَوْ جَاءَ أَحَدٌ مِّنكُم مِّنَ الْغَائِطِ أَوْ لَامَسْتُمُ النِّسَاءَ فَلَمْ تَجِدُوا مَاءً فَتَيَمَّمُوا صَعِيدًا طَيِّبًا فَامْسَحُوا بِوُجُوهِكُمْ وَأَيْدِيكُم مِّنْهُ ۚ مَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيَجْعَلَ عَلَيْكُم مِّنْ حَرَجٍ …

"O you who believe, when you rise for prayer, wash your faces and your hands to the elbows, wipe your heads, and [wash/wipe] your feet to the ankles. If you are in a state of *janābah*, purify yourselves. If you are ill or travelling, or one of you comes from the privy, or you have 'touched' women, and you find no water, then seek clean earth and wipe your faces and hands with it. God does not wish to place hardship on you…"

**Interpretive points:**

1. **Feet: washed or wiped?** In this text *arjulakum* is accusative. That makes it grammatically parallel to *wujūhakum* (washed), although it is placed after the wiping clause. A genitive reading would attach it to *ruʾūsikum* (wiped). The Qur'an alone cannot decide which vocalisation is authoritative. On the Hafs text, **washing** is the more natural syntax.
2. ***Ilā* ("to the elbows/ankles")**: inclusive or exclusive. **Ambiguous.**
3. ***Lāmastum al-nisāʾ***: "touched women", or a euphemism for intercourse. Compare 2:237 *min qabli an tamassūhunna*, where *mass* clearly means consummation. **Ambiguous.**
4. ***Janābah*** (major impurity) requires *iṭṭahharū*. 4:43 specifies *ḥattā taghtasilū*, "until you bathe". The causes of *janābah* are **not defined**. Sexual intercourse is the natural reading of the word.
5. **Menstruation.** 2:222 forbids intercourse during menstruation. The Qur'an **does not** say that menstruating women are excused from prayer or fasting. **Unspecified.**
6. **What breaks ablution.** Only "coming from the privy" and "touching women" are listed, in the context of tayammum. Whether sleep, bleeding, etc. break ablution is **unspecified**.
7. **Frequency.** *Idhā qumtum ilā al-ṣalāh*, "when you rise to pray", can mean before every prayer, or only when one is not already in a state of purity. **Ambiguous.**

**Tayammum** (dry ablution) is **explicit**: 4:43 and 5:6, with clean earth, face and hands.

### 3.7 Direction (qiblah)

2:142–150:

- 2:144 *fa-walli wajhaka shaṭra al-masjidi al-ḥarām; wa-ḥaythu mā kuntum fa-wallū wujūhakum shaṭrah*, "turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque; wherever you are, turn your faces toward it".
- Repeated in 2:149–150.
- Qualifiers: 2:115 *fa-aynamā tuwallū fa-thamma wajhu Allāh*, "wherever you turn, there is the face of God". 2:177 says piety is not turning east or west.

**Classification:** facing the Sacred Mosque: **explicit**. *Shaṭr* ("direction/side") allows approximation. 2:115 suggests the direction is not absolute when it cannot be determined. This is **strongly inferable**, but 2:115 does not mention prayer explicitly.

**The earlier qiblah.** 2:143: *wa-mā jaʿalnā al-qiblata allatī kunta ʿalayhā illā li-naʿlama man yattabiʿu al-rasūl*, "We did not appoint the qiblah you used to face except to know who would follow the Messenger". God here claims to have **appointed** a previous direction. No verse of the Qur'an records that appointment. See §9 and §13: this is one of the strongest internal indications that divine instruction to the Prophet existed outside the recited text.

### 3.8 Congregation and Friday

- **Congregation.**
  - 2:43 *wa-rkaʿū maʿa al-rākiʿīn*, "bow with those who bow".
  - 4:102 assumes a group praying behind the Prophet: *fa-aqamta lahum al-ṣalāh*, "you lead/establish the prayer for them".
  - 62:9 assumes a public gathering.
  - **Classification:** whether congregational prayer is obligatory is **ambiguous**. "Bow with those who bow" may mean "join the community of worshippers" rather than "pray in congregation". Note that 3:43 gives the same command to Mary.
- **Friday.** 62:9–11:

  > يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِن يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَذَرُوا الْبَيْعَ … فَإِذَا قُضِيَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَانتَشِرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ

  "When the call is made to prayer on the day of assembly, hasten to the remembrance of God and leave trade… when the prayer is completed, disperse in the land."

  - **Explicit:** a prayer on the day of *jumuʿah*, preceded by a call, for which trade must stop.
  - **Unspecified:** its time, its form, whether it replaces another prayer, and whether a sermon is part of it. 62:11 says people left the Prophet *qāʾiman* (standing). A standing address is suggested, not stated.
- **The call to prayer.** 5:58: *wa-idhā nādaytum ilā al-ṣalāti ttakhadhūhā huzuwan*, "when you call to prayer, they take it in mockery". A public call existed. Its wording is **unspecified**.

### 3.9 Travel, danger, illness

| Situation | Verse | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Danger | 2:239 | *fa-in khiftum fa-rijālan aw rukbānā*: pray walking or riding. When safe, *fa-dhkurū Allāha kamā ʿallamakum mā lam takūnū taʿlamūn*, "remember God as He taught you what you did not know" |
| Travel + fear | 4:101 | *fa-laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an taqṣurū min al-ṣalāti in khiftum*, "no blame in shortening the prayer if you fear" |
| Battle | 4:102 | Split congregation: one group prays with the Prophet while the other guards. After "they prostrate", they withdraw and the other group comes |
| After danger | 4:103 | Remember God standing, sitting, lying; when secure, *fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh* (perform it in full) |
| Illness | 4:43, 5:6, 73:20 | Tayammum; lighter night recitation for the sick |

**Observations:**

- 4:101 permits shortening **conditional on fear** (*in khiftum*). Whether shortening during safe travel is allowed is **unspecified** at best, and the condition cuts against it.
- "Shorten *from* the prayer" presupposes a known baseline length. The length is never stated.
- 2:239's *kamā ʿallamakum*: God taught the believers the form of remembrance/prayer. **Where** He taught it is not stated. That is a pointer to instruction outside the text, or to a pedagogy the text does not record.

### 3.10 Missed prayers

There is no make-up provision. The Qur'an condemns those who "neglect" (*aḍāʿū*, 19:59) or are "heedless of" (*sāhūn*, 107:5) prayer. **Unspecified.** Contrast fasting, where make-up is explicit (2:184).

### 3.11 The fullest Qur'an-only prayer routine

Built strictly from the above:

1. **Purify.** Wash face, hands to elbows, wipe head, wash (or wipe) feet to ankles (5:6). Bathe if in *janābah* (4:43). Use clean earth on face and hands if there is no water or you are ill (4:43, 5:6). Clean clothes (74:4 *wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir*, possibly metaphorical). Adorn yourself at every place of worship (7:31 *khudhū zīnatakum ʿinda kulli masjid*).
2. **Be sober and attentive** (4:43; 23:2 *khāshiʿūn*; 107:4–6 against showing off).
3. **Face the Sacred Mosque** (2:144).
4. **Stand devoutly** (2:238), with God's name remembered and magnified (87:15, 17:111).
5. **Recite** from the Qur'an, at moderate volume (17:110, 73:20).
6. **Bow and prostrate** (22:77). Number and order unspecified, with standing–bowing–prostrating suggested by 22:26.
7. **Times:** at minimum dawn and night (24:58), plus the ends of the day and the span from the sun's decline to dark (11:114, 17:78). Optionally the night vigil (17:79, 73:1–20).
8. **Friday:** stop trade at the call and attend (62:9).
9. **Fear/travel:** shorten, pray walking or riding, or in shifts (2:239, 4:101–102).

**Elements of contemporary prayer that cannot be independently established from the Qur'an:**

- Five fixed daily prayers (ambiguous, not explicit).
- Unit counts (2/4/3/4/2).
- The fixed sequence within a unit.
- Mandatory al-Fātiḥah.
- The opening *takbīr* formula.
- Specific words in bowing, prostration and sitting.
- The sitting testimony and its wording.
- The closing salutation.
- Raising hands and the position of hands.
- Silent versus audible prayers by time.
- The wording of the call to prayer.
- The Friday sermon's structure.
- Make-up rules.
- Shortening during safe travel.
- Combining prayers.
- Precise time windows.
- Menstrual exemption.
- The full list of what nullifies ablution.

## 4. Fasting (ṣawm)

### 4.1 Core text: 2:183–187

> 2:183 يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ

"Fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may be mindful."

> 2:184 أَيَّامًا مَّعْدُودَاتٍ ۚ فَمَن كَانَ مِنكُم مَّرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ ۚ وَعَلَى الَّذِينَ يُطِيقُونَهُ فِدْيَةٌ طَعَامُ مِسْكِينٍ ۖ فَمَن تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَّهُ ۚ وَأَن تَصُومُوا خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ

"A counted number of days. Whoever of you is ill or travelling, then a number of other days. Upon those who *yuṭīqūnahu*, a redemption: feeding a poor person. Whoever volunteers good, it is better for him. And that you fast is better for you."

> 2:185 شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ الَّذِي أُنزِلَ فِيهِ الْقُرْآنُ … فَمَن شَهِدَ مِنكُمُ الشَّهْرَ فَلْيَصُمْهُ ۖ وَمَن كَانَ مَرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍ فَعِدَّةٌ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ ۗ يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ بِكُمُ الْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ الْعُسْرَ وَلِتُكْمِلُوا الْعِدَّةَ وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَاكُمْ

"The month of Ramadan, in which the Qur'an was sent down… so whoever of you *shahida* the month, let him fast it… God wants ease for you, not hardship, and that you complete the count and magnify God for guiding you."

> 2:187 أُحِلَّ لَكُمْ لَيْلَةَ الصِّيَامِ الرَّفَثُ إِلَىٰ نِسَائِكُمْ … عَلِمَ اللَّهُ أَنَّكُمْ كُنتُمْ تَخْتَانُونَ أَنفُسَكُمْ فَتَابَ عَلَيْكُمْ … وَكُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكُمُ الْخَيْطُ الْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ الْخَيْطِ الْأَسْوَدِ مِنَ الْفَجْرِ ۖ ثُمَّ أَتِمُّوا الصِّيَامَ إِلَى اللَّيْلِ ۚ وَلَا تُبَاشِرُوهُنَّ وَأَنتُمْ عَاكِفُونَ فِي الْمَسَاجِدِ

"Permitted to you on the night of the fast is intimacy with your wives… God knew you were betraying yourselves, so He turned to you… eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct from the black thread of dawn, then complete the fast until night. Do not be intimate with them while you are in retreat in the mosques."

### 4.2 Findings

| Question | Answer | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| When? | The month of Ramadan (2:185). It is the only month the Qur'an names | Explicit |
| Whole month? | *Fa-l-yaṣumhu*, "let him fast it", and *li-tukmilū al-ʿiddah*, "complete the count" | Explicit / strongly inferable |
| Daily start | When the white thread is distinct from the black thread "of dawn" | Explicit (wording); exact moment ambiguous |
| Daily end | *Ilā al-layl*, "until night" | Explicit; whether "night" begins at sunset or at full dark is **ambiguous** |
| Prohibited | Eating, drinking (by implication of the night permission), sexual intimacy (*rafath*) | Strongly inferable |
| Other invalidators (smoking, medicine, injections, swallowing, vomiting) | Not mentioned | Unspecified |
| Exempt | The ill and travellers, who make up the days later | Explicit |
| *Yuṭīqūnahu* | See 4.3 | Ambiguous |
| Make-up | "A number of other days" | Explicit; timing unspecified |
| Start/end of month | *Man shahida al-shahr*. 2:189: the new moons are *mawāqīt*, "time-markers" | Strongly inferable that it is lunar; method of determining the month unspecified |
| Ritual at the end | *Wa-li-tukabbirū Allāh*, magnify God at completion | Explicit (an act of glorification); no festival, prayer or charity specified |
| Night prayer in Ramadan | None tied to Ramadan | Unspecified |
| *Laylat al-qadr* | 97:1–5, 44:3: a blessed night when revelation descended. No ritual is attached | Explicit (its existence); date unspecified |
| Retreat (*iʿtikāf*) | Mentioned (2:187; 2:125 *al-ʿākifīn*); no rule beyond no intimacy | Explicit mention, unspecified procedure |
| Voluntary fasts | *Fa-man taṭawwaʿa khayran* (2:184). No named voluntary fast days | Unspecified |

### 4.3 The *yuṭīqūnahu* problem

The root ṭ-w-q gives *ṭāqah*, "capacity" (2:249 *lā ṭāqata lanā al-yawm*, "we have no strength today"; 2:286 *mā lā ṭāqata lanā bih*). *Yuṭīqūnahu* most directly means "those able to do it".

- **Reading A:** those able to fast may pay a redemption (*fidyah*) instead. Supported by the direct grammar and by the immediately following *wa-an taṣūmū khayrun lakum*, "and fasting is better for you". That phrase only makes sense if not fasting was an option for some capable people.
  - *Problem:* 2:185 then says *fa-man shahida minkum al-shahra fa-l-yaṣumhu*, without repeating the redemption option. That suggests either an internal tightening or a different scope.
- **Reading B:** "those for whom it is an exhausting strain" (the form-IV verb taken as bearing capacity at its limit). Supported by the sense of the exemption pattern. Not supported by the plain dictionary sense of the form.

Neither reading is decisive. The relation between 2:184 and 2:185 is **potentially contradictory**, or at minimum shows a two-stage structure the text does not explain. A Qur'an-only community would have to choose between:

1. A permanent redemption option for anyone.
2. A redemption option only for the chronically unable.
3. An option that 2:185 superseded. This assumes abrogation, a doctrine the Qur'an alludes to (2:106) but never applies to a named verse.

### 4.4 The prior-restriction implication in 2:187

*ʿAlima Allāhu annakum kuntum takhtānūna anfusakum fa-tāba ʿalaykum… fa-l-āna bāshirūhunna*, "God knew you were betraying yourselves… so **now** be intimate with them". This implies that a stricter practice had been in force, probably no intimacy on fast nights, and that the believers had been breaching it. **That earlier rule is not in the Qur'an.** It may have been self-imposed or inherited ("as prescribed for those before you"). The text does not say. Compare the earlier qiblah (2:143). The pattern is that the Qur'an modifies rules it never states.

### 4.5 Other fasts in the Qur'an

| Verse | Purpose | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 2:196 | Hajj redemption / no sacrificial animal | 3 days in Hajj + 7 on return = 10 |
| 4:92 | Manslaughter without means to free a slave | 2 consecutive months (*shahrayni mutatābiʿayn*) |
| 5:89 | Broken oath, if unable to feed/clothe/free | 3 days |
| 5:95 | Killing game while in pilgrim sanctity | "Equivalent" in fasting (*ʿadlu dhālika ṣiyāman*) |
| 58:4 | *Ẓihār* | 2 consecutive months |
| 19:26 | Mary's vow | *Ṣawm* as **abstention from speech**: *innī nadhartu li-l-raḥmāni ṣawman fa-lan ukallima al-yawma insiyyā* |

19:26 matters semantically. *Ṣawm* in the Qur'an means abstention as such, not specifically abstention from food. The food/drink/sex content of the Ramadan fast comes from 2:187's permission structure, not from the word itself.

### 4.6 Fasting vs prayer: level of detail

Fasting is **better specified than prayer** on the dimensions that matter for performance:

- **Fasting:** month, daily start and end, prohibited acts, exemptions, make-up, redemption.
- **Prayer:** has purity rules and direction, but lacks count, sequence and words.

A Qur'an-only community could perform Ramadan in a way that is broadly recognisable. Disagreement would come at the edges: the time of sunset vs dark, month determination, the scope of redemption, and modern invalidators.

## 5. Zakat and charity

### 5.1 The term *zakāh*

- **Range.** *Zakāh* appears paired with *ṣalāh* throughout: 2:43, 2:83, 2:110, 2:177, 2:277, 4:77, 4:162, 5:12, 5:55, 9:5, 9:11, 9:18, 9:71, 19:31, 19:55, 21:73, 22:41, 22:78, 24:37, 24:56, 27:3, 31:4, 33:33, 58:13, 73:20, 98:5.
- **Root meaning.** z-k-w/y: purity and growth.
  - 91:9 *qad aflaḥa man zakkāhā*, "successful is the one who purifies it [the self]".
  - 87:14–15 *qad aflaḥa man tazakkā, wa-dhakara isma rabbihi fa-ṣallā*, "successful is the one who purifies himself, remembers his Lord's name, and prays". This mirrors the *zakāh/ṣalāh* pairing.
  - 18:74 *nafsan zakiyyah*, "a pure soul".
- **Given by earlier prophets and peoples** (19:31 Jesus, 19:55 Ishmael, 21:73, 2:83, 5:12).
- **Present in early Meccan contexts** without any administration: 41:7 *alladhīna lā yuʾtūna al-zakāh*; 23:4 *wa-lladhīna hum li-l-zakāti fāʿilūn*. Note that 23:4 uses *fāʿilūn*, "doers of", which fits "purification" as well as "alms". **Ambiguous.**

### 5.2 Rates, thresholds, frequency

- **Percentage.** None for *zakāh*. The only explicit fraction for distributing wealth is **one-fifth of war gains** (8:41: *fa-anna li-llāhi khumusahu wa-li-l-rasūli wa-li-dhī al-qurbā wa-l-yatāmā wa-l-masākīni wa-bni al-sabīl*).
- **Threshold.** None.
- **Frequency.** None, except that 6:141 attaches a due to harvest: *wa-ātū ḥaqqahu yawma ḥaṣādih*, "give its due on the day of its harvest". The amount is not given.
- **Measure of what to give.**
  - 2:219: *wa-yasʾalūnaka mādhā yunfiqūn, quli al-ʿafw*, "they ask what to spend; say: the surplus".
  - 17:29 and 25:67: moderation, neither tight-fisted nor fully open-handed.
- **"A known right."**
  - 70:24–25: *wa-lladhīna fī amwālihim ḥaqqun maʿlūm, li-l-sāʾili wa-l-maḥrūm*, "in whose wealth is a **known** right for the asker and the deprived".
  - 51:19: *wa-fī amwālihim ḥaqqun li-l-sāʾili wa-l-maḥrūm*, the same with **no** *maʿlūm*.
  - The adjective "known" points to a determinate amount familiar to the audience **but not stated in the text** (see §13).

**Classification:**

- Giving from wealth as a regular duty paired with prayer: **explicit**.
- Fixed rate, threshold, or annual cycle: **unspecified**.
- Due on harvest: **explicit**, amount unspecified.

### 5.3 Recipients

9:60:

> إِنَّمَا الصَّدَقَاتُ لِلْفُقَرَاءِ وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَالْعَامِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا وَالْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَفِي الرِّقَابِ وَالْغَارِمِينَ وَفِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ ۖ فَرِيضَةً مِّنَ اللَّهِ

"The *ṣadaqāt* are only for the poor, the needy, those employed to administer them, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing necks [captives/slaves], debtors, in God's cause, and the wayfarer: an obligation from God."

**Key observations:**

- **The verse says *ṣadaqāt*, not *zakāh*.** The Qur'an never assigns recipients to the word *zakāh*. Identifying the eight categories as *zakāh* recipients is an inference: both are obligatory giving, and 9:103 links *ṣadaqah* to purification (*tuzakkīhim*). It is a **strong** inference, but it is an inference.
- **"Those employed to administer them"** implies a collection system. 9:103 *khudh min amwālihim ṣadaqah*, "take from their wealth a charity", gives the Prophet a collecting role. Who holds that role after him is **unspecified**.
- **Other recipient lists, overlapping but not identical:**
  - 2:177: kin, orphans, needy, wayfarer, askers, captives.
  - 2:215: parents, kin, orphans, needy, wayfarer.
  - 4:36.
  - 8:41: *khums*.
  - 59:7: *fayʾ*, with the stated purpose *kay lā yakūna dūlatan bayna al-aghniyāʾi minkum*, "so that it does not circulate only among the rich".

### 5.4 *Zakāh*, *ṣadaqah*, *infāq*

| Term | Qur'anic profile |
|---|---|
| *Zakāh* | Paired with prayer. Purification. Ancient. Never quantified. Never given recipients by name |
| *Ṣadaqah / ṣadaqāt* | Can be obligatory (9:60 *farīḍah*), collected (9:103), voluntary (2:271 open or secret), or expiatory (2:196, 58:12–13). Must not be spoiled by reminders or injury (2:264) |
| *Infāq* | Generic spending in God's way (2:3, 2:261–274, 57:7) |

The Qur'an distinguishes *zakāh* (an institution paired with prayer) from *infāq* and *ṣadaqah* (acts of giving) in its usage patterns, but it never defines the difference. **Ambiguous.**

### 5.5 Debt, interest, inheritance, poverty

- **Usury / interest (*ribā*).** Prohibited: 2:275 *wa-aḥalla Allāhu al-bayʿa wa-ḥarrama al-ribā*; 2:278–279 "war from God"; 3:130 *aḍʿāfan muḍāʿafah* ("doubled and redoubled"); 30:39. What exactly counts as *ribā* (any interest? compounding?) is **ambiguous**. 3:130's qualifier is descriptive or restrictive, depending on the reader.
- **Debt.**
  - 2:282 is the Qur'an's longest verse. It requires written documentation of deferred debts, a just scribe, dictation by the debtor (or a guardian), and two male witnesses or one man and two women.
  - 2:283: a pledge when travelling.
  - 2:280: give an insolvent debtor time; remitting the debt is better.
  - **Explicit and highly procedural.**
- **Inheritance.** See §7.4.
- **Poverty.**
  - The poor are entitled to a share (*ḥaqq*), not merely to kindness (17:26 *wa-āti dhā al-qurbā ḥaqqahu wa-l-miskīn*; 30:38).
  - Feeding the poor is part of righteousness (90:14–16; 107:1–3; 69:34; 89:18).

**Of modern zakat practice:**

- **Can be established:** an obligation, recipient categories (via *ṣadaqāt*), harvest dues, the one-fifth on war gains.
- **Unresolved:** the rate on wealth, the minimum threshold, the annual cycle, which assets are liable, the end-of-Ramadan charity, and the post-prophetic collecting authority.

## 6. Hajj

### 6.1 The obligation

3:96–97:

> إِنَّ أَوَّلَ بَيْتٍ وُضِعَ لِلنَّاسِ لَلَّذِي بِبَكَّةَ مُبَارَكًا وَهُدًى لِّلْعَالَمِينَ ۝ فِيهِ آيَاتٌ بَيِّنَاتٌ مَّقَامُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ ۖ وَمَن دَخَلَهُ كَانَ آمِنًا ۗ وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا

"The first House set up for people is the one at Bakkah, blessed and a guidance… In it are clear signs: the station of Abraham. Whoever enters it is safe. Pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to God by people who are able to find a way to it."

- **Explicit:**
  - Hajj to the House is a duty for those able.
  - It must be completed for God (2:196 *wa-atimmū al-ḥajja wa-l-ʿumrata li-llāh*).
- **Frequency:** **unspecified**. Once in a lifetime or more is not stated.
- **Ability (*istiṭāʿah*):** **undefined**.
- ***ʿUmrah*** (2:158, 2:196): a distinct rite, **undefined**.

### 6.2 Timing

- 2:189 *qul hiya mawāqītu li-l-nāsi wa-l-ḥajj*: the new moons are time-markers for people and for Hajj.
- 2:197 *al-ḥajju ashhurun maʿlūmāt*: "Hajj is [in] well-known months". The months are **not named**. The plural *ashhur* implies three or more, or at least more than one.
- 9:36: twelve months, four of them sacred (*arbaʿatun ḥurum*). They are **not named**.
- 9:37 forbids *nasīʾ*, the postponement or intercalation of sacred months.
- 2:203 *wa-dhkurū Allāha fī ayyāmin maʿdūdāt; fa-man taʿajjala fī yawmayni fa-lā ithma ʿalayh*: remember God on "counted days". Hurrying in two days is no sin, and neither is staying longer.
- 22:28: *fī ayyāmin maʿlūmāt*, "on known days".
- 9:3: *yawm al-ḥajj al-akbar*, "the day of the greater pilgrimage". **Undefined.**

**Classification:** Hajj is lunar and occurs in known months: **explicit**. Which months and days: **unspecified**. The text explicitly delegates this to what is "known".

| Safa and Marwa | 2:158 *inna al-ṣafā wa-l-marwata min shaʿāʾiri Allāh… fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhi an yaṭṭawwafa bihimā* | "No blame" in going round them | Explicit permission; obligation **ambiguous** ("no blame" is a permission formula, though it can answer a specific scruple) |
| ʿArafāt | 2:198 *fa-idhā afaḍtum min ʿarafātin fa-dhkurū Allāha ʿinda al-mashʿari al-ḥarām* | Presupposes being there and leaving it | Presence is **strongly inferable**; duration and timing unspecified |
| *Al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām* | 2:198 | Remember God there | Explicit; location undefined |

| *Tafath* | 22:29 *thumma l-yaqḍū tafathahum* | Hapax; probably "their grooming/unkemptness" | **Ambiguous** (lexically underdetermined) |

### 6.4 Restrictions during pilgrim sanctity

- **Conduct.** 2:197: *fa-man faraḍa fīhinna al-ḥajja fa-lā rafatha wa-lā fusūqa wa-lā jidāla fī al-ḥajj*, "no sexual relations, no wrongdoing, no quarrelling in Hajj". **Explicit.**
- **Hunting.**
  - Forbidden while *ḥurum* (5:1, 5:95–96).
  - Sea game is permitted (5:96).
  - Hunting is allowed again after leaving sanctity (5:2 *wa-idhā ḥalaltum fa-ṣṭādū*).
  - **Penalty (5:95):** an equivalent animal judged by *dhawā ʿadlin minkum*, "two just persons among you". Alternatives: feeding the poor or an equivalent fast. **Explicit, with a built-in human adjudication mechanism.**
- **Hair.** No shaving until the offering reaches its place, unless ill or with a head ailment, in which case a redemption of fasting, charity or sacrifice (2:196). **Explicit.** The amounts are unspecified.
- **Ritual garments (*iḥrām*).** **Absent.** The text uses *ḥurum* (a state) but says nothing of garments, unstitched cloth, perfume, or covering the head.
- **Blocked pilgrims (*iḥṣār*).** If prevented, send what offering is easy (2:196). **Explicit.**
- **Combining ʿumrah with Hajj (*tamattuʿ*).** One who combines them owes an offering, or 3+7 days of fasting, if their family is not resident at the Sacred Mosque (2:196). **Explicit and unusually detailed.**

### 6.5 Abrahamic framing

- 2:125–129 and 22:26–27: Abraham and Ishmael raised the House and purified it "for those who circle, stand, bow and prostrate".
- Abraham proclaimed the Hajj (22:27 *wa-adhdhin fī al-nāsi bi-l-ḥajji yaʾtūka rijālan wa-ʿalā kulli ḍāmir*).
- 2:128: *wa-arinā manāsikanā*, "**show** us our rites". The rites were *shown*, not dictated in text.
- 16:123: *ittabiʿ millata ibrāhīma ḥanīfā*, "follow the creed/way of Abraham".

### 6.6 A Qur'an-only Hajj itinerary

1. In the known Hajj months, travel to the House at Bakkah/Mecca (3:96–97; 2:197).
2. Enter a state of sanctity (*ḥurum*). From that point:
   - no sex, wrongdoing or quarrelling;
   - no hunting land game;
   - no shaving.
3. Bring a sacrificial animal if able, garlanded or not (5:2, 5:97).
4. Circle the House (22:29); go between Safa and Marwa (2:158); pray at the station of Abraham (2:125).
5. Be at ʿArafāt, press on from there, and remember God at al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām (2:198–199).
6. On the known days, mention God's name over the animals and sacrifice them. Eat, and feed the poor (22:28, 22:36).
7. Complete grooming (*tafath*), fulfil vows, circle the Ancient House (22:29).
8. Shave or shorten (48:27).
9. Remember God on the counted days, leaving after two days or staying longer (2:203).

**Missing:**

- Which days.
- The order of ʿArafāt relative to *ṭawāf*.
- The number of circuits and of walks between Safa and Marwa.
- Garments.
- Stoning.
- Minā.
- The night stay.
- The farewell circuit.
- What ʿumrah consists of.
- Whether women have different rules.
- The location of the Mashʿar and the boundaries of ʿArafāt.

**Assessment.** The Qur'an gives a recognisable *skeleton* of Hajj and assumes a living practice to fill it in. 2:199 openly says to follow where "the people" go. This is the most explicit case in the Qur'an of ritual procedure being **delegated to communal practice**.

## 7. Family law and everyday obligations

### 7.1 Marriage and consent

- **Mutual agreement.** 2:232: *fa-lā taʿḍulūhunna an yankiḥna azwājahunna idhā tarāḍaw baynahum bi-l-maʿrūf*, "do not prevent them from marrying their husbands if they agree between themselves in the recognised way".
  - Women are the grammatical subject of *yankiḥna*, "they marry".
  - Third parties are forbidden to obstruct them.
  - Mutual agreement (*tarāḍaw*) is the condition.
- **Against coercion.** 4:19: *lā yaḥillu lakum an tarithū al-nisāʾa karhā*, "it is not lawful for you to inherit women against their will".
- **Guardianship tension.**
  - 2:221: *wa-lā tunkiḥū al-mushrikīna ḥattā yuʾminū*, "do not marry [your women] to polytheist men". The causative form IV implies that men give women in marriage.
  - 4:25: slave women marry *bi-idhni ahlihinna*, "with their people's permission".
  - The Qur'an therefore holds both women's agency (2:232, 2:230 *ḥattā tankiḥa zawjan ghayrah*) and a family role. Whether a guardian is **required**: **ambiguous**.
- **Prohibited degrees.** 4:22–24, an explicit list:
  - fathers' wives;
  - mothers, daughters, sisters, aunts, nieces;
  - milk-mothers and milk-sisters;
  - mothers-in-law;
  - stepdaughters in one's care, if the marriage with their mother was consummated;
  - sons' wives;
  - two sisters at once;
  - married women (except *mā malakat aymānukum*).
- **Interfaith.**
  - 5:5: men may marry chaste women of the People of the Book.
  - 2:221: no marriage with polytheists, either sex.
  - 60:10: believing women are not lawful to disbelievers.
  - Whether believing women may marry men of the People of the Book is **unspecified**. The text is silent; 60:10's *kuffār* may or may not include them.
- **Dowry.**
  - 4:4: *wa-ātū al-nisāʾa ṣaduqātihinna niḥlah*, "give women their dowries as a gift".
  - 4:24: *fa-ātūhunna ujūrahunna farīḍah*.
  - 2:236–237: half the dowry is due on divorce before consummation.
  - 4:20: do not take back even a "heap of gold" (*qinṭār*).
  - **Explicit** obligation. Amount: **unspecified**.

### 7.2 Polygyny

4:3:

> وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا تُقْسِطُوا فِي الْيَتَامَىٰ فَانكِحُوا مَا طَابَ لَكُم مِّنَ النِّسَاءِ مَثْنَىٰ وَثُلَاثَ وَرُبَاعَ ۖ فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا فَوَاحِدَةً أَوْ مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ

"If you fear you will not deal justly with orphans, then marry such women as seem good to you, two, three, four. If you fear you will not be just, then one, or what your right hands possess."

- **Conditional framing.** The permission is introduced by a condition about **orphans**. Whether polygyny is unconditionally permitted, or permitted only in the context of justice to orphans, is **ambiguous**.
- **Maximum number.** *Mathnā wa-thulātha wa-rubāʿ* are distributive forms ("in twos, threes, fours"). A ceiling of four is the natural reading. An additive reading (2+3+4) is grammatically strained. Four is **strongly inferable**.
- **Tension with 4:129.** *Wa-lan tastaṭīʿū an taʿdilū bayna al-nisāʾi wa-law ḥaraṣtum*, "you will never be able to be just between women, however much you try". Read alone, 4:3 + 4:129 could make the condition impossible to meet. But 4:129 continues: *fa-lā tamīlū kulla al-mayl*, "so do not incline entirely [to one]". That presupposes plural marriage continuing under a lower standard. Two kinds of justice are in play: *taʿdilū* in 4:3 and in 4:129. **Potentially contradictory**, but resolvable from the text if they are distinguished as practical vs emotional justice. That distinction is itself an inference.
- **The Prophet.** 33:50–52 give him specific marital provisions and then a limit (*lā yaḥillu laka al-nisāʾu min baʿd*).

### 7.3 Divorce, waiting periods, reconciliation

- **Arbitration before divorce.** 4:35: *fa-bʿathū ḥakaman min ahlihi wa-ḥakaman min ahlihā*, "send an arbiter from his family and one from hers". **Explicit.**
- **Marital discord.**
  - 4:34: wife's *nushūz* → admonish, separate in beds, *wa-ḍribūhunna*.
  - 4:128: husband's *nushūz* → reconciliation.
  - *Ḍaraba* has a broad range in the Qur'an:
    - strike: 8:12;
    - travel: 4:101 *ḍarabtum fī al-arḍ*;
    - set forth an example: 14:24 *ḍaraba Allāhu mathalan*;
    - draw a cover: 24:31 *wa-l-yaḍribna bi-khumurihinna*;
    - turn away: 43:5 *a-fa-naḍribu ʿankum al-dhikra ṣafḥan*.
  - "Strike" is the most direct reading with a personal object in the accusative, but the text does not close off alternatives. **Ambiguous.**
- **Waiting periods (*ʿiddah*).** **Explicit**, with the definition of *qurūʾ* **ambiguous**.

  | Case | Waiting period | Verse |
  |---|---|---|
  | Divorced woman | *Thalāthata qurūʾ* (3 menstrual cycles or 3 purity intervals; the word occurs only here) | 2:228 |
  | Post-menopausal, or women who have not menstruated (*wa-llāʾī lam yaḥiḍna*) | 3 months | 65:4 |
  | Pregnant | Until delivery | 65:4 |
  | Widow | 4 months and 10 days | 2:234 |
  | Divorced before consummation | No waiting period | 33:49 |

- **Divorce process.**
  - 2:229: *al-ṭalāqu marratān; fa-imsākun bi-maʿrūfin aw tasrīḥun bi-iḥsān*, "divorce is twice; then retain honourably or release kindly".
  - 2:230: after a third, remarriage only after she marries another husband.
  - 65:1: divorce *li-ʿiddatihinna*, count the period, do not expel them from their homes.
  - 65:2: at the end, retain or part, and *ashhidū dhaway ʿadlin minkum*, take two just witnesses.
- **Wife-initiated release.** 2:229: *fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā fīmā aftadat bih*, "no blame on them in what she gives to free herself". **Explicit** in principle; procedure unspecified.
- **Maintenance.**
  - 2:241: provision for divorced women.
  - 65:6–7: house them according to means; pay for nursing.
  - 2:236: gift on divorce before consummation.
- **Widow's provision vs waiting period.**
  - 2:240: provision for a year without expulsion.
  - 2:234: 4 months and 10 days.
  - They concern different things: a waiting period vs a bequest of residence. They can be harmonised, but the text does not do it. **Potentially contradictory** if both are read as waiting periods.
- **Special procedures.**
  - *Ẓihār* (58:2–4): expiation by freeing a slave, else 2 months of consecutive fasting, else feeding 60 poor.
  - *Īlāʾ*, an oath of abstention (2:226): 4 months.
  - *Liʿān*, mutual imprecation (24:6–9).
  - All **explicit and procedural**.

### 7.4 Inheritance (4:7, 4:11–12, 4:176; 2:180; 2:240)

- **Basic rules: explicit.**
  - Men and women both inherit (4:7).
  - A son receives twice a daughter's share (*li-l-dhakari mithlu ḥaẓẓi al-unthayayn*).
  - Fixed fractions are given for daughters, parents, spouses, and siblings in *kalālah*.
  - Shares are distributed after bequests and debts (*min baʿdi waṣiyyatin yūṣī bihā aw dayn*).
- **Bequest.** 2:180 *kutiba ʿalaykum… al-waṣiyyatu li-l-wālidayni wa-l-aqrabīn*: a bequest to parents and relatives is **prescribed**. No upper limit on bequests is stated.
- **Gaps:**
  1. **Exactly two daughters.** 4:11 gives 2/3 to daughters *fawqa ithnatayn*, "above two", and 1/2 to one daughter. The share of exactly two daughters is **unspecified** in 4:11. 4:176 gives two **sisters** 2/3, which supports 2/3 by analogy. That is an inference.
  2. **Over-subscription.**
     - Example: wife 1/8 + two parents 1/6 each + more than two daughters 2/3 = 3/24 + 8/24 + 16/24 = **27/24**.
     - The fixed fractions can exceed the estate.
     - The Qur'an gives **no rule** for reducing the shares. **Potentially contradictory / unspecified.**
  3. **Siblings in *kalālah*.**
     - 4:12: a brother or sister gets 1/6, more than one share 1/3.
     - 4:176: a sister gets 1/2, two sisters 2/3, and brothers take double a sister's share.
     - Both are about *kalālah*, yet they give different fractions.
     - The text does not distinguish types of sibling. **Potentially contradictory** unless one assumes the two verses mean different sibling classes, which is not stated.
  4. **Grandchildren, residuaries, the definition of *kalālah*** (4:176 defines it partially: *in imruʾun halaka laysa lahu waladun*): **unspecified or ambiguous**.
- **Assessment.** The most mathematically specified area of the Qur'an, and also the one with the clearest internal arithmetic gap.

### 7.5 Sexual misconduct

- **Flogging.** 24:2: *al-zāniyatu wa-l-zānī fa-jlidū kulla wāḥidin minhumā miʾata jaldah*, a hundred lashes. **Explicit.**
- **Proof and slander.**
  - 24:4: four witnesses are required.
  - False accusers receive 80 lashes, and their testimony is never accepted again.
  - 24:13 repeats the four-witness requirement. **Explicit.**
- **Earlier provision.** 4:15–16: confinement of women "until death takes them or God makes a way for them"; punishment for two men (or a man and a woman) *fa-ādhūhumā*, "harm/reprove them both". Relation to 24:2: **ambiguous** (supersession, or different offences).
- **Divisible punishment.** 4:25: slave women receive *niṣfu mā ʿalā al-muḥṣanāti mina al-ʿadhāb*, half the punishment of free married women. 33:30 doubles the punishment for the Prophet's wives. A halvable and doublable punishment is consistent with a number of lashes and **inconsistent with execution**.
  - Within the Qur'an, the only stated penalty for *zinā* is flogging. **Explicit.**
  - Any death penalty for it is **absent** from the text.
- **Avoiding sexual immorality.**
  - 17:32: do not approach *zinā*.
  - 24:3: marriage restrictions for *zunāt*.

### 7.6 Children, nursing, orphans, vulnerable kin

- **Nursing and gestation.**
  - 2:233: mothers nurse for *ḥawlayni kāmilayni li-man arāda an yutimma al-raḍāʿah*, two full years for those who wish to complete it. The father provides maintenance *bi-l-maʿrūf*. Weaning by mutual consultation (*ʿan tarāḍin minhumā wa-tashāwur*). Wet-nursing is permitted.
  - 46:15: *wa-ḥamluhu wa-fiṣāluhu thalāthūna shahrā*, gestation plus weaning is thirty months. Combined with 2:233 (24 months of nursing), this gives a **minimum gestation of six months**. This is derived purely from the text by arithmetic, and it is **strongly inferable**.
  - 31:14: *wa-fiṣāluhu fī ʿāmayn*.
- **Parents.**
  - 17:23–24: honour parents and not even say "uff".
  - 29:8 and 31:15: do not obey them in associating partners with God.
- **Infanticide.** Forbidden: 6:151, 17:31, 81:8–9.
- **Orphans.**
  - 4:2: give them their property.
  - 4:5–6:
    - test them until marriageable age;
    - if mature judgement (*rushd*) is perceived, hand over their property;
    - take witnesses.
  - 4:10: consuming orphans' wealth is eating fire.
  - 2:220: improve their affairs.
  - 6:152, 17:34.
  - 93:9 and 107:2: do not oppress or repel them.
  - **Explicit and procedural.**
- **Adoption.** 33:4–5: call them by their fathers' names; adoptive sons are not sons. **Explicit.**

### 7.7 Food and slaughter

- **Prohibited (core list).** 2:173, 5:3, 6:145, 16:115:
  - carrion;
  - blood (6:145: *daman masfūḥan*, "blood poured forth");
  - pork;
  - what is dedicated to other than God.
- **Further items in 5:3.** Animals that are:
  - strangled, beaten, fallen, or gored;
  - partly eaten by predators, *illā mā dhakkaytum*, "except what you ritually slaughter";
  - sacrificed on stone altars.

  Also forbidden: divining by arrows.
- **Exhaustiveness claim.** 6:145:

  > قُل لَّا أَجِدُ فِي مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيَّ مُحَرَّمًا عَلَىٰ طَاعِمٍ يَطْعَمُهُ إِلَّا أَن يَكُونَ مَيْتَةً أَوْ دَمًا مَّسْفُوحًا أَوْ لَحْمَ خِنزِيرٍ … أَوْ فِسْقًا أُهِلَّ لِغَيْرِ اللَّهِ بِهِ

  "Say: I do not find in what has been revealed to me anything forbidden to one who eats, except carrion, poured blood, pork… or a perversion dedicated to other than God."

  The Prophet is made to say that the prohibitions known to him **from revelation** are exhaustive. This is one of the strongest Qur'an-only texts (see §9).
- **Against self-made prohibitions.** 5:87; 6:140; 6:148–150; 7:32 (*qul man ḥarrama zīnata Allāh*); 10:59; 16:116; 66:1. The Qur'an repeatedly condemns prohibiting what God has not prohibited.
- **Slaughter.**
  - God's name must be mentioned (6:118, 6:121: *wa-lā taʾkulū mimmā lam yudhkari smu Allāhi ʿalayh*).
  - The animal must be ritually slaughtered (*dhakkā*, 5:3).
  - For *budn*: standing in rows (*ṣawāff*); eat once "their sides fall" (22:36).
  - **The method** (throat, which vessels, implements): **unspecified**, beyond what the verb *dhabaḥa* implies (cutting).
- **Other foods.**
  - Food of the People of the Book is lawful (5:5).
  - Game caught by trained animals is lawful (5:4).
  - Sea food is lawful (5:96).
- **Necessity.** Exemption for one compelled, not desiring nor transgressing (2:173, 5:3, 6:145).
- **Animals not listed** (predators, birds of prey, donkeys, etc.): **unspecified**. 6:145 implies they are permitted. 7:157 *yuḥarrimu ʿalayhim al-khabāʾith*, "he forbids them impure things", introduces an undefined category.

### 7.8 Intoxicants and gambling

The texts in sequence:

- 2:219: *fīhimā ithmun kabīrun wa-manāfiʿu li-l-nās, wa-ithmuhumā akbaru min nafʿihimā*, "in them is great sin and some benefit; their sin is greater".
- 4:43: not drunk at prayer.
- 5:90–91: *innamā al-khamru wa-l-maysiru wa-l-anṣābu wa-l-azlāmu rijsun min ʿamali al-shayṭāni fa-jtanibūh*, "intoxicants, gambling, altars and divining arrows are filth from Satan's work, so **avoid** it". 5:91 adds *fa-hal antum muntahūn*, "will you not desist?".

**Is it forbidden?**

- **Objection:** some argue that *ijtanibū* ("avoid") is weaker than *ḥarrama* ("forbade").
- **Internal answer:** the Qur'an supplies a syllogism.
  - 7:33: *qul innamā ḥarrama rabbiya al-fawāḥisha… wa-l-ithma*, "my Lord has forbidden… **sin**".
  - 2:219: in *khamr* there is *ithmun kabīr*, great sin.
  - Therefore *khamr* falls under 7:33's prohibition.
- *Ijtanibū* is also used for idolatry and false speech (22:30: *fa-jtanibū al-rijsa mina al-awthāni wa-jtanibū qawla al-zūr*), which shows it is not a weak word.
- **Conclusion:** prohibition is **strongly inferable**.

**The scope of *khamr*.** The root kh-m-r means "to cover". The same root gives *khumur*, women's coverings, in 24:31. If *khamr* means "what covers the mind", it extends to all intoxicants. This is lexically motivated, and **strongly inferable**. 16:67 distinguishes *sakar* (intoxicant) from *rizqan ḥasanā* (good provision), implying intoxicants are not a good provision.

### 7.9 Dress and modesty

- **Men.** 24:30: lower the gaze, guard the private parts. No garment specification.
- **Women.** 24:31:
  - lower the gaze, guard the private parts;
  - *wa-lā yubdīna zīnatahunna illā mā ẓahara minhā*, "not display their adornment except what appears of it";
  - *wa-l-yaḍribna bi-khumurihinna ʿalā juyūbihinna*, "draw their *khumur* over their *juyūb* (chest openings)";
  - a list of relatives before whom adornment may be shown;
  - do not stamp the feet to reveal hidden adornment.
- **Key words.**
  - *Khimār* = "a covering", from the root "to cover". The verse **presupposes** that women already wore *khumur* and directs them **to the chest**. Whether a *khimār* covers the head is **not stated** in the Qur'an. A head-covering is the most common referent of the word in Arabic, but that is outside-the-text lexicography.
  - *Illā mā ẓahara minhā* (what ordinarily appears): **ambiguous**.
- **Outer garments.** 33:59: the Prophet's wives, daughters and believing women should draw their *jalābīb* over themselves *dhālika adnā an yuʿrafna fa-lā yuʾdhayn*, "so they are recognised and not harmed". A **purpose-clause** (recognition/protection) is attached.
- **Elderly women.** 24:60: those past childbearing may set aside their garments, without displaying adornment.
- **The screen (*ḥijāb*).** 33:53 refers to a screen for the Prophet's wives, not a garment.
- **General principle.** 7:26: *libās al-taqwā dhālika khayr*, "the garment of mindfulness is best".

**Classification:**

- Modest conduct for both sexes: **explicit**.
- Covering the chest with a *khimār*: **explicit**.
- Covering the hair: **ambiguous / unspecified** from the text alone.
- Face covering: **unspecified**.

### 7.10 Hygiene

- 2:222: avoid intercourse during menstruation until purification. *Innallāha yuḥibbu al-tawwābīna wa-yuḥibbu al-mutaṭahhirīn*.
- 9:108: *rijālun yuḥibbūna an yataṭahharū*.
- 74:4: *wa-thiyābaka fa-ṭahhir*, "purify your garments".
- Circumcision, toilet etiquette, grooming norms: **absent**.

### 7.11 Business

- 2:275: trade is lawful, usury forbidden.
- 4:29: *illā an takūna tijāratan ʿan tarāḍin minkum*, trade by mutual consent.
- 5:1: *awfū bi-l-ʿuqūd*, fulfil contracts.
- 2:282: write it down.
- 17:35, 26:181–183, 55:9, 83:1–3: full weights and measures.
- 62:9: stop trade at the Friday call.
- 2:188: no bribing judges.
- 4:58: return trusts.

**Explicit** principles, with a documentation procedure. Contract types, partnerships and pricing are unspecified.

### 7.12 Oaths and promises

- 2:224–225: do not make God's name an excuse against doing good. Unintentional oaths are not held against you.
- 5:89 gives an explicit expiation scale for broken deliberate oaths:
  1. feed ten poor people at average family standard;
  2. or clothe them;
  3. or free a slave;
  4. if unable, fast three days.
- 16:91–92: keep covenants.
- 66:2: *qad faraḍa Allāhu lakum taḥillata aymānikum*, God has ordained the dissolution of your oaths.
- 17:34: *wa-awfū bi-l-ʿahd*.

### 7.13 Social conduct and ethics

- **Social conduct: a dense set of explicit norms.**
  - Ask permission before entering houses (24:27–29).
  - Times for children and servants to seek permission (24:58–59).
  - Return greetings with better ones (4:86).
  - Verify news (49:6).
  - No mockery, name-calling, suspicion, spying or backbiting (49:11–12).
  - Reconcile believers (49:9–10).
  - Speak kindly (2:83; 17:53).
  - Walk humbly (17:37; 25:63; 31:18–19).
  - Gentle voice (31:19).
- **Core ethical texts.**
  - The ethical "decalogue" of 17:22–39.
  - 6:151–153.
  - 25:63–76.
  - 4:36: good to parents, kin, orphans, the needy, neighbours near and far, companions, travellers, and those under your authority.
  - 4:135 and 5:8: justice even against oneself and toward enemies.
  - 16:90: justice, excellence, giving to kin; forbidding indecency, wrong, transgression.
- **Criminal law.**
  - Theft: 5:38, cut the hands. *Qaṭaʿa* can also mean "wound": 12:31 *qaṭṭaʿna aydiyahunna*, intensive form II. No value threshold.
  - Brigandage: 5:33.
  - Retaliation with remission and blood-money: 2:178–179, 5:45.
  - Manslaughter: 4:92, compensation *diyatun musallamah*, amount unspecified.
- **Slavery** is assumed and regulated:
  - 4:25, 4:36;
  - 24:33 contracts of emancipation (*fa-kātibūhum in ʿalimtum fīhim khayrā*);
  - manumission is praised as expiation and charity (90:13 *fakku raqabah*; 9:60).
  - The text does not abolish it. It provides many exits from it.

**Assessment of §7.** Family and transactional law is the **most legally complete** domain of the Qur'an. It is more specified than ritual. A Qur'an-only community could run a functioning family-law system. It would need to legislate:

- amounts (dowry, blood-money, maintenance);
- the definition of *qurūʾ*;
- the inheritance arithmetic gaps;
- evidentiary procedure beyond the stated witness numbers;
- penalty thresholds (theft).

## 8. The Qur'anic portrait of Muhammad

### 8.1 Identity

- **Named "Muhammad" four times:** 3:144, 33:40, 47:2, 48:29.
- **"Aḥmad":** once, in Jesus' announcement (61:6 *wa-mubashshiran bi-rasūlin yaʾtī min baʿdī smuhu aḥmad*). Whether that refers to Muhammad is an identification the text implies but does not state.
- **Usual address:** *yā ayyuhā al-nabī* or *yā ayyuhā al-rasūl*, or the second-person singular.

### 8.3 Character and limitations

- **Praised.**
  - *Wa-innaka la-ʿalā khuluqin ʿaẓīm*, "you are of tremendous character" (68:4).
  - Gentle; consults (3:159 *wa-shāwirhum fī al-amr*).
  - Grieves over people's suffering (9:128).
- **Rebuked or corrected.**
  - 80:1–10: frowning at a blind man.
  - 9:43: *ʿafā Allāhu ʿanka lima adhinta lahum*, permitting hypocrites to stay behind.
  - 8:67–68: taking captives.
  - 66:1: forbidding himself what God allowed.
  - 33:37: *wa-tukhfī fī nafsika mā Allāhu mubdīhi wa-takhshā al-nās*, hiding something God would reveal, out of fear of people.
  - 17:73–75: nearly inclined toward opponents.
  - 18:23–24: not to promise without "if God wills".
  - 4:105–107: not to be an advocate for traitors.
  - 6:52: not to drive away the poor.
  - Commanded to seek forgiveness for his *dhanb* (40:55; 47:19; 48:2).
- **Limits of his knowledge and power.**
  - 6:50 and 7:188: does not know the unseen and has no treasures.
  - 46:9: *mā adrī mā yufʿalu bī wa-lā bikum*, "I do not know what will be done with me or you".
  - 18:110 and 41:6: *innamā ana basharun mithlukum yūḥā ilayy*, "I am a human like you; it is revealed to me".
  - 88:21–22: *lasta ʿalayhim bi-muṣayṭir*, "you are not a controller over them".
  - 28:56: *innaka lā tahdī man aḥbabt*, "you cannot guide whom you love".
- **Miracles.**
  - The text repeatedly declines to supply them and points to the Book as sufficient (17:59, 17:90–93, 29:50–51).
  - 54:1 *iqtarabati al-sāʿatu wa-nshaqqa al-qamar*, "the Hour drew near and the moon split". It is ambiguous whether this is a past event or eschatological.

### 8.4 Assessment

**Reconstructable:**

- A theological portrait: role, message, character, and the limits of his authority.
- A partial **episodic** history: emigration, named and unnamed battles, domestic tensions, the community's internal conflict with hypocrites.

**Not reconstructable:**

- His parents' or grandparents' names, and his clan.
- His date of birth, his age at the call, the length of his mission, and its chronology.
- His wives' names and number, and his children's names.
- Any companion except Zayd.
- The identity of the "companion in the cave".
- The order of battles beyond what 3:123 and 33 imply.
- The causes and course of most events.
- His death.

The Qur'anic Muhammad is a **prophet defined by his function**. He is not a biographical subject. The text is addressed *to* him far more than it narrates *about* him. The fuller figure Muslims know depends entirely on sources outside the Qur'an.

## 9. Religious authority and obedience to the Messenger

### 9.1 The texts

**A. Obedience commands**

- 3:32, 3:132: *aṭīʿū Allāha wa-l-rasūl*. A single verb governs both.
- 4:59, 5:92, 24:54, 47:33, 64:12: *aṭīʿū Allāha wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūl*. The verb is repeated.
- 4:59, full text:

  > يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَأُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنكُمْ ۖ فَإِن تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ

  "Obey God, obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. If you dispute about anything, refer it to God and the Messenger."

  - Disputes are referred to "God and the Messenger".
  - "Those in authority" are **not** included in that referral.
- 4:80: *man yuṭiʿi al-rasūla fa-qad aṭāʿa Allāh*, "whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God".
- 4:64: *wa-mā arsalnā min rasūlin illā li-yuṭāʿa bi-idhni Allāh*, "We sent no messenger except to be obeyed by God's permission". This is a general principle for **all** messengers.
- 4:65:

  > فَلَا وَرَبِّكَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ حَتَّىٰ يُحَكِّمُوكَ فِيمَا شَجَرَ بَيْنَهُمْ ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُوا فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ حَرَجًا مِّمَّا قَضَيْتَ وَيُسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا

  "No, by your Lord, they do not believe until they make you judge in their disputes and then find no resistance within themselves to what you decide, and submit fully."

- 24:63: *fa-l-yaḥdhari alladhīna yukhālifūna ʿan amrih*, "let those who oppose his command beware".
- 33:36: *mā kāna li-muʾminin wa-lā muʾminatin idhā qaḍā Allāhu wa-rasūluhu amran an yakūna lahum al-khiyaratu min amrihim*, "no believer has a choice once God and His Messenger have decided a matter".
- 59:7: *wa-mā ātākum al-rasūlu fa-khudhūhu wa-mā nahākum ʿanhu fa-ntahū*, "whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; whatever he forbids you, refrain". The context is the distribution of *fayʾ* (abandoned enemy property).
- 3:31: *qul in kuntum tuḥibbūna Allāha fa-ttabiʿūnī*, "if you love God, follow me".
- 7:157–158: follow the unlettered prophet, who *yaʾmuruhum bi-l-maʿrūf… wa-yuḥillu lahum al-ṭayyibāt wa-yuḥarrimu ʿalayhim al-khabāʾith*, "commands them right… makes lawful the good and forbids the foul".
- 9:29: *wa-lā yuḥarrimūna mā ḥarrama Allāhu wa-rasūluh*, "they do not forbid what God and His Messenger have forbidden".
- 33:21: *la-qad kāna lakum fī rasūli Allāhi uswatun ḥasanah*, "in God's Messenger you have an excellent example".
- 24:62: believers do not leave a collective matter (*amr jāmiʿ*) without his permission.

**B. Limits on the Messenger's role**

- The duty is "only clear delivery":
  - 5:99: *mā ʿalā al-rasūli illā al-balāgh*.
  - Also 3:20, 5:92, 13:40, 16:35, 16:82, 24:54, 29:18, 42:48, 64:12.
- 6:50, 10:15, 46:9: *in attabiʿu illā mā yūḥā ilayy*, "I follow only what is revealed to me".
- 10:15: *qul mā yakūnu lī an ubaddilahu min tilqāʾi nafsī*, "it is not for me to change it of my own accord".
- 69:44–47: if he had fabricated sayings against God, God would have seized him.
- 66:1: rebuked for forbidding what God made lawful.
- 6:114: *a-fa-ghayra Allāhi abtaghī ḥakaman wa-huwa alladhī anzala ilaykumu al-kitāba mufaṣṣalā*, "shall I seek a judge other than God, when He sent down to you the Book detailed?".
- 6:145: the Prophet's knowledge of food prohibitions is bounded by "what has been revealed to me".
- 42:21: *am lahum shurakāʾu sharaʿū lahum mina al-dīni mā lam yaʾdhan bihi Allāh*, "do they have partners who legislated religion for them without God's permission?".
- 7:3: *ittabiʿū mā unzila ilaykum min rabbikum wa-lā tattabiʿū min dūnihi awliyāʾ*, "follow what was sent down to you from your Lord, and follow no patrons besides Him".

**C. The Book's self-description**

- *Mufaṣṣal*, detailed (6:114; 11:1 *kitābun uḥkimat āyātuhu thumma fuṣṣilat*; 41:3).
- *Tibyānan li-kulli shayʾ*, a clarification of everything (16:89).
- *Tafṣīla kulli shayʾ*, a detailing of everything (12:111; also 6:154, 7:145 of Moses' tablets).
- 6:38: *mā farraṭnā fī al-kitābi min shayʾ*, "We have neglected nothing in the Book". In context, *al-kitāb* may be the cosmic record: the verse is about animals and birds being communities.
- 29:51: *a-wa-lam yakfihim annā anzalnā ʿalayka al-kitāba yutlā ʿalayhim*, "is it not sufficient for them that We sent down the Book recited to them?". In context this answers demands for miracles.
- 45:6: *fa-bi-ayyi ḥadīthin baʿda Allāhi wa-āyātihi yuʾminūn*, "in what discourse after God and His signs will they believe?". Also 77:50 and 7:185.
- 5:3: *al-yawma akmaltu lakum dīnakum*, "today I have perfected your religion".
- 25:30: the Messenger complains that his people *ittakhadhū hādhā al-qurʾāna mahjūrā*, "treated this Qur'an as something abandoned".

| 4:113 | *wa-anzala Allāhu ʿalayka al-kitāba wa-l-ḥikmata wa-ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam* | "The Book **and** the wisdom", plus "taught you what you did not know" |

### 9.2 Analysis: what does obedience to the Messenger cover?

**Three distinct things are being asked:**

1. **(O1)** Obedience to the Messenger **during his lifetime**, in his concrete commands and judgements.
2. **(O2)** Obedience to the **message he delivered**, i.e. the Qur'an.
3. **(O3)** A **continuing** obligation, after his death, to follow instructions of his that are not in the Qur'an.

**(O1) is explicit.**

- 4:65 (*yuḥakkimūka*, "make **you** judge"), 4:64, 24:62–63, 33:36, 58:12–13 and 9:103 all describe a living person deciding disputes, distributing property, granting leave and collecting charity.
- These are not reducible to "reciting the Qur'an".
- The Qur'an therefore **does** establish authority beyond recitation **during his lifetime**.
- Pure "messenger = postman" readings are contradicted by 4:65 and 33:36.

**(O2) is explicit.**

- 4:80's equation of obeying the Messenger with obeying God holds at least for the delivered message.
- 5:99 and its parallels restrict his **duty** to delivery. Note that they restrict what he is **accountable for**: "if you turn away, his burden is delivery; yours is yours" (24:54). They do **not** restrict what believers must obey. This distinction is often missed. 24:54 itself pairs *aṭīʿū al-rasūl* with *mā ʿalā al-rasūli illā al-balāgh* **in the same verse**. So the Qur'an does not see "only delivery" and "obey him" as contradictory.

**(O3) is not settled by the text.** The arguments on both sides, from the Qur'an alone:

*For a continuing obligation beyond the text:*

- **(a)** The Qur'an itself shows that non-textual divine instruction existed: 2:143, 66:3, 8:7, 59:5, 2:239. So "everything God wanted followed is in the Qur'an" is not stated by the Qur'an. It is contradicted, at least for the Prophet's lifetime.
- **(b)** *Al-kitāb wa-l-ḥikmah* (2:129, 2:151, 2:231, 3:164, 4:113, 62:2) can be read as two things: the Book, plus something else.
- **(c)** 33:21 sets him as an *uswah*, a pattern to be followed. A pattern of conduct is more than a text.
- **(d)** The Qur'an presupposes ritual practice that it does not describe (§3, §6). If the presupposed practice was obligatory, and the Qur'an says it was (*kitāban mawqūtā*, *li-llāhi ʿalā al-nāsi ḥijju al-bayt*), then **some** extra-textual content is necessary for compliance.
- **(e)** 16:44 *li-tubayyina li-l-nāsi mā nuzzila ilayhim*, "so that you **make clear** to people what was sent down to them". This can be read as an explanatory mandate.

*Against a continuing obligation beyond the text, or for limiting it:*

- **(a′)** Every passage in (a) concerns communication **to the Prophet** for **his time**: a qiblah later superseded, a private household matter, a battle promise, a tactical permission. None is presented as standing law for later believers. Several were **superseded** (the qiblah) or **one-off** (66:3, 8:7, 59:5).
- **(b′)** *Ḥikmah* is, at least in part, Qur'anic content:
  - 17:22–38 is a list of commands, closed by 17:39: *dhālika mimmā awḥā ilayka rabbuka mina al-ḥikmah*, "**that** is from what your Lord revealed to you of the wisdom".
  - 33:34: *wa-dhkurna mā yutlā fī buyūtikunna min āyāti Allāhi wa-l-ḥikmah*. Wisdom is **recited** (*yutlā*).
  - 2:231: *wa-mā anzala ʿalaykum mina al-kitābi wa-l-ḥikmati yaʿiẓukum bih*. "What He sent down… of the Book and the wisdom" is singular in its effect.
  - *Ḥikmah* is also given to Luqman (31:12), David (2:251), and Jesus (3:48, 5:110). That shows it is not a Muhammad-specific corpus.
  - So (b) is not established. *Ḥikmah* **may** include more than the Qur'an, but the text does not require it.
- **(c′)** *Uswah* occurs three times:
  - 33:21: Muhammad.
  - 60:4: Abraham, *qad kānat lakum uswatun ḥasanatun fī ibrāhīma wa-lladhīna maʿah*.
  - 60:6.
  - Abraham's example is accessible to believers **only through the Qur'an's narrative**. 60:4 even specifies an exception from it (*illā qawla ibrāhīma li-abīhi la-astaghfiranna lak*).
  - 33:21 sits in the battle of the Confederates (33:9–27). It concerns steadfastness under siege.
  - So the *uswah* concept does not by itself imply a non-Qur'anic corpus.
- **(d′)** The presupposed practices **pre-dated** Muhammad (prayer 8:35, 21:73; fasting 2:183; Hajj 2:199, 22:27). The Qur'an points to an **Abrahamic communal practice** (16:123; 2:128 *arinā manāsikanā*) as their referent, not specifically to the Prophet's personal instructions. A Qur'an-only position can hold that the Qur'an presupposes *inherited communal practice*. That is a different external source from a *corpus of prophetic sayings*. But this still concedes that the Qur'an is not procedurally self-sufficient.
- **(e′)** *Bayyana* in the Qur'an is repeatedly the **opposite of *katama*** (to conceal):
  - 2:159: *inna alladhīna yaktumūna mā anzalnā mina al-bayyināti wa-l-hudā min baʿdi mā bayyannāhu li-l-nās*.
  - 3:187: *la-tubayyinunnahu li-l-nāsi wa-lā taktumūnah*, said of the People of the Book's obligation toward their own scripture.
  - So *li-tubayyina* in 16:44 can mean "to **make public / manifest**", as opposed to conceal, rather than "to add explanatory content".
  - 75:19 *thumma inna ʿalaynā bayānah*, "then upon **Us** is its explanation", assigns *bayān* to God.
  - Both readings of 16:44 are lexically available. **Ambiguous.**
- **(f′)** **Sunnah vocabulary.**
  - The Qur'an uses *sunnah* for:
    - *sunnat Allāh*, God's way (33:38, 33:62, 35:43, 48:23, 40:85);
    - *sunnat al-awwalīn*, the precedent of earlier peoples (8:38, 15:13, 18:55).
  - 17:77, *sunnata man qad arsalnā qablaka min rusulinā wa-lā tajidu li-sunnatinā taḥwīlā*, speaks of the treatment of messengers and calls it "**Our** sunnah".
  - The phrase "sunnah of the Messenger/Prophet" **does not occur**.
  - This is a vocabulary fact. It does not prove that no such authority exists, because the concept could exist under other words (*uswah*, *ittibāʿ*). It does show that the Qur'an does not formulate the doctrine in those terms.
- **(g′)** ***Ḥadīth* vocabulary.** The Qur'an calls **itself** *aḥsan al-ḥadīth* (39:23). It challenges opponents: *fa-bi-ayyi ḥadīthin baʿdahu yuʾminūn* (77:50; also 7:185; 45:6). This is the single strongest lexical argument for a Qur'an-only position. Its limits:
  - In context, the verses address unbelievers' rejection of revelation as such, not intra-believer reliance on prophetic reports.
  - *Ḥadīth* here means "discourse/report" in general.

### 9.3 The decisive hinge

The Qur'an establishes **(O1)** and **(O2)** clearly. On **(O3)** it gives:

- **No verse** stating that, after the Prophet's death, believers must follow his non-Qur'anic instructions.
- **No verse** stating that they must **not**.
- **No mechanism** for preserving or authenticating non-Qur'anic instructions. Contrast 15:9 and 75:17, which assert divine preservation and collection of the *dhikr* / *qurʾān*.
- **Present-tense, presence-based framing** for the judicial authority (4:64 *jāʾūka*, "came to you"; 4:65 *yuḥakkimūka*). The Prophet's mortality is explicitly anticipated (3:144) with no succession instructions attached.

**Conclusion.** Whether obedience to the Messenger requires following practices not contained in the Qur'an is **not determinable from the text alone**:

- The text **establishes** extra-textual prophetic authority during his life.
- The text **is silent** about its transmission after his death.
- An answer on either side requires a premise from outside the Qur'an:
  - either "lifetime authority implies a preserved, continuing corpus";
  - or "only what God undertook to preserve (the Qur'an) remains binding".

### 9.4 Earlier scriptures and messengers

- The Qur'an *confirms* earlier scriptures (*muṣaddiqan li-mā bayna yadayh*) and *guards* over them (*muhayminan ʿalayh*) (5:48).
- People of the Torah and Gospel are to judge by what God revealed in them (5:43–47).
- 5:48: *li-kullin jaʿalnā minkum shirʿatan wa-minhājā*, "for each of you We appointed a law and a way". Legal diversity across communities is acknowledged.
- 42:13 asserts a common core of religion (*al-dīn*) from Noah to Muhammad.
- The milla of Abraham is to be followed (16:123; 3:95; 2:130–135).
- Earlier scriptures can confirm the message (10:94: *fa-sʾali alladhīna yaqraʾūna al-kitāba min qablik*).
- 16:43 and 21:7: *fa-sʾalū ahla al-dhikri in kuntum lā taʿlamūn*, "ask the people of the reminder if you do not know".

So the Qur'an itself points outward to other religious knowledge-holders on certain questions. That cuts against a strictly closed "Qur'an alone" epistemology. It also shows that the Qur'an anticipates **non-Qur'anic, non-prophetic** knowledge sources.

### 9.5 Disagreement and its resolution

- **Refer disputes to God and the Messenger** (4:59; 42:10 *wa-mā khtalaftum fīhi min shayʾin fa-ḥukmuhu ilā Allāh*).
- **Refer matters of security/news to the Messenger and those in authority**, "those who can derive it would know it" (4:83 *la-ʿalimahu alladhīna yastanbiṭūnahu minhum*). This is a textual basis for *istinbāṭ* (inference) by qualified people.
- **A group to gain understanding in religion** (9:122 *li-yatafaqqahū fī al-dīn*). This is a textual basis for specialists.
- **Consultation:** 42:38 *wa-amruhum shūrā baynahum*; 3:159.
- **Just arbiters:** 4:35, 5:95.
- **Do not split into sects:** 3:103, 3:105, 6:159, 30:31–32. But 5:48 and 11:118 (*wa-lā yazālūna mukhtalifīn*) accept that some disagreement is permanent, and 5:48 says *ilā Allāhi marjiʿukum… fa-yunabbiʾukum bimā kuntum fīhi takhtalifūn*: God will judge the differences finally.
- **Muḥkam / mutashābih.** 3:7: the Book contains *muḥkamāt* (firm verses), the "mother of the Book", and *mutashābihāt* (ambiguous verses). Pursuing the ambiguous ones to make mischief is condemned. The Qur'an **admits** internal ambiguity.

## 11. Reconstructing everyday Qur'an-only life

The model below is **one** plausible community, labelled **"Community A"**. It is followed by the main axes on which other Qur'an-only communities would legitimately differ.

### 11.1 A day

- **Before dawn.**
  - She wakes. If she was intimate with her spouse, she bathes (4:43). Otherwise she washes face, arms to elbows, wipes her head, washes her feet (5:6). If water is scarce or she is ill, she wipes face and hands with clean earth.
  - She faces Mecca (2:144). The direction is approximate (*shaṭr*). A compass app is a practical aid the text neither requires nor forbids.
- **Dawn prayer (*ṣalāt al-fajr*, named in 24:58).**
  - She stands, magnifies God, and recites a passage of the Qur'an at moderate volume (17:110, 17:78).
  - She bows and prostrates. Community A uses the 22:26 order (stand, bow, prostrate) and repeats it twice, by its own convention. It openly labels the count as a community choice, not a Qur'anic requirement.
  - She may say *subḥāna rabbī* in prostration, drawing on the Qur'anic vocabulary of *tasbīḥ* (87:1 *sabbiḥi sma rabbika al-aʿlā*; 56:74). This is an **adopted**, not prescribed, formula.
- **Day.**
  - At work: fair weights and measures (83:1–3); documented debts (2:282); kept contracts (5:1); no interest-bearing loans (2:275). What counts as interest is a live internal debate.
  - Speech: no backbiting, verify information before sharing it (49:6, 49:12).
  - Dress: modest; she covers her chest (24:31). Community A's women cover their hair, treating *khimār* as a head-covering by ordinary Arabic usage. A neighbouring Qur'an-only community does not, arguing the text specifies only the chest.
- **Afternoon / sunset.**
  - Community A prays at the sun's decline and at sunset, reading 17:78 and 11:114 as "the ends of the day".
  - It thus has three fixed prayers: dawn, sunset/dusk, night. Plus *tasbīḥ* at midday and afternoon (30:17–18) as remembrance rather than full prayer.
- **Food.**
  - No pork, carrion, poured blood, or meat dedicated to other than God.
  - Meat from Jews and Christians is accepted (5:5).
  - She says God's name before eating meat (6:118, 6:121).
  - No alcohol or recreational intoxicants (5:90; root kh-m-r).
  - Other foods are unrestricted. 6:145 is taken as exhaustive.
- **Night.** *Ṣalāt al-ʿishāʾ* (24:58). Optionally a voluntary night vigil with longer recitation (73:20; 17:79).
- **Before sleep.** Remembrance "lying on their sides" (3:191). She asks forgiveness (3:17 *wa-l-mustaghfirīna bi-l-asḥār*).

### 11.2 A week

- **Friday.** At the call to prayer, she closes her shop (62:9). She attends a communal prayer with a standing address (62:11 is suggestive). Afterwards she returns to business (62:10).
- **Form of the Friday gathering.** Community A decides it by consultation (42:38).
- **Weekday gatherings.** There is no obligation to attend other prayers in congregation, though "bow with those who bow" (2:43) is read as an encouragement.

### 11.3 Ramadan

- **Start and end.** The month begins with the new crescent (2:189). Community A uses naked-eye sighting. Another community uses astronomical calculation; both cite *mawāqīt* and *shahida al-shahr*.
- **The daily fast.**
  - Eating stops when the dawn's thread is distinct (2:187).
  - Food, drink and intimacy are avoided until "night".
  - Community A breaks fast at sunset. A minority waits for visible darkness, arguing that *layl* is darkness.
- **Exemptions.** The ill and travellers make up the days later.
- ***Fidyah*.** Community A allows it only for the chronically unable (reading B of §4.3). Another community allows it more broadly (reading A).
- **Menstruating women.** The Qur'an does not exempt them.
  - Community A lets them choose, by analogy to illness (2:184).
  - Another community requires them to fast.
- **Night recitation.** Extended recitation at night (73:20) is encouraged. It is not a fixed congregational institution.
- **Month's end.** Collective *takbīr* (2:185). Community A adds a voluntary charity drive and a communal meal by its own decision. These are labelled as community custom.

### 11.4 Zakat

- **Rate.** Community A sets a rate by consultation, anchored in two texts:
  - 2:219 *al-ʿafw*, giving the surplus beyond needs;
  - 8:41's one-fifth as the only Qur'anic percentage.
  - Debates are explicit: one faction argues for 1/5 of surplus income; another for an unfixed "known right" (70:24) of 2–10% set yearly.
- **Recipients.** Distributed to the eight categories of 9:60.
- **Harvest.** Farmers give at harvest (6:141).

### 11.5 Hajj

Pilgrims travel in the months the community regards as "known" (2:197). Community A adopts the lunar months traditionally used, and admits that this identification rests on continuous practice, not on the text.

The sequence:

1. Enter sanctity: no sex, quarrelling, or hunting.
2. Circle the House (number by community convention).
3. Go between Safa and Marwa.
4. Gather at ʿArafāt.
5. Remember God at the Mashʿar.
6. Sacrifice, feeding the poor.
7. Shave or shorten.
8. Circle again.
9. Remember God on the counted days, leaving after two or three.

There is no stoning ritual, since the Qur'an does not mention one. A Qur'an-only community performing Hajj physically in Mecca would in practice be performing it alongside the majority community's ritual. This is a real practical constraint the text cannot resolve.

### 11.6 Relationship to God and the Prophet

- **God.** Direct and unmediated. 2:186: *wa-idhā saʾalaka ʿibādī ʿannī fa-innī qarīb, ujību daʿwata al-dāʿi idhā daʿān*, "when My servants ask about Me, I am near; I answer the caller". 50:16: closer than the jugular vein.
- **The Prophet.** Revered as the final prophet (33:40), the bringer of the Book, a moral exemplar in steadfastness (33:21). They invoke blessings on him (33:56). They do not attribute to him laws beyond the Qur'an. They regard the Qur'an's corrections of him (80:1–10; 66:1) as part of his authentic portrait.

### 11.7 Where Qur'an-only communities would diverge

| Axis | Plausible positions |
|---|---|
| Daily prayers | 2 / 3 / 5 |
| Units | 1 / 2 / variable / free |
| Formulas | Free recitation / adopted Qur'anic phrases / inherited formulas kept as non-binding custom |
| Feet in ablution | Wash / wipe |
| Safe-travel shortening | Yes / no |
| Fast end | Sunset / darkness |
| *Fidyah* scope | Broad / narrow / abrogated |
| Menstruation and worship | Exempt / not exempt / optional |
| Zakat rate | Fixed by consultation / individual conscience / surplus principle |
| Hair covering | Required / not required |
| Polygyny | Permitted up to four / only in an orphan-care context / effectively barred by 4:129 |
| Guardian in marriage | Required / not required |
| Hajj months | Inherited calendar / reconstructed from text (some argue the four sacred months) |
| Intoxicants | Prohibited (majority inference) / discouraged (minority, if the 7:33 syllogism is rejected) |
| Inheritance gaps | Proportional reduction / priority rules / court discretion |
| Authority | Strict "Qur'an only" / "Qur'an + inherited practice" (*milla* of Abraham) |

## 12. Internal consistency and unresolved questions

### 12.1 Can essential worship be reconstructed without external instructions?

**Partly.**

| Rite | Reconstructable without external instruction? |
|---|---|
| Fasting | Yes, almost fully |
| Prayer | Its **existence, preconditions, direction and components** yes. Its **form** no |
| Hajj | Its **skeleton** yes. Its **schedule and counts** no |
| Zakat | Its **purpose and recipients** yes. Its **rate** no |

The text's own design explains the gap: it repeatedly points to things already "known" (*maʿlūm*) and to what "the people" do (2:199).

### 12.2 Can law be applied consistently without procedural detail?

**Not uniformly.** Application requires human discretion, and the Qur'an builds discretion in:

- just arbiters (4:35, 5:95);
- witnesses (2:282, 65:2);
- consultation (42:38);
- "the recognised" (*al-maʿrūf*) as a standard, used dozens of times in family law: 2:228, 2:229, 2:231, 2:232, 2:233, 2:234, 2:236, 2:240, 2:241, 4:6, 4:19, 4:25, 65:2, 65:6.

**The *maʿrūf* standard is the text's own answer to procedural gaps.** It delegates to shared moral-customary understanding. Consistency **across** communities is therefore not guaranteed and was apparently not intended (5:48).

### 12.3 How much interpretive discretion is required?

| Domain | Discretion required |
|---|---|
| Ritual form | High |
| Ritual existence and timing | Moderate |
| Fasting | Low |
| Family law | Moderate to low |
| Food | Low |
| Criminal penalties | Moderate (thresholds and evidence) |
| Finance | Moderate (definition of interest; rates) |

### 12.4 Would two Qur'an-only communities practise identical rituals?

**No.** For prayer and Hajj specifically, the text leaves too many free parameters. Convergence would happen only if the communities shared an external reference, such as inherited practice.

### 12.5 Mechanisms for resolving disagreement, from the Qur'an alone

1. **Refer to God's revelation** (4:59, 42:10). This works only where the text is determinate.
2. **Consultation** (42:38).
3. **Specialists** (9:122, 4:83).
4. **Ease and capacity:**
   - 2:185 *yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusr*;
   - 2:286 *lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā*;
   - 22:78 *wa-mā jaʿala ʿalaykum fī al-dīni min ḥaraj*;
   - 64:16 *fa-ttaqū Allāha mā staṭaʿtum*.
   - These are tie-breakers toward the less burdensome reading.
5. **Silence as pardon.** 5:101:

   > يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَسْأَلُوا عَنْ أَشْيَاءَ إِن تُبْدَ لَكُمْ تَسُؤْكُمْ … عَفَا اللَّهُ عَنْهَا

   "Do not ask about things which, if made plain to you, would trouble you… God has pardoned it."

   Read with the cow episode (2:67–71), where repeated questions narrowed the requirement and increased the burden, this suggests that **unspecified matters are deliberately left open**. This is the strongest text-internal argument that procedural gaps are a **design feature**, not a defect. Its **limit:** it explains silence about *details*. It does not explain commands whose *core referent* is undefined (e.g. "the known months").
6. **Tolerated permanent disagreement**, with final judgement by God (5:48; 22:69).

### 12.6 Does the Qur'an distinguish obligatory from optional?

**Sometimes, with explicit markers:**

| Marker | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *Kutiba ʿalaykum* | Prescribed | 2:178, 2:180, 2:183, 2:216 |
| *Farīḍah* / *faraḍa* | Obligation | 4:11, 4:24, 9:60, 2:197 |
| *Ḥarrama* / *ḥurrimat* | Prohibition | 4:23, 5:3, 6:151, 7:33 |
| *Nāfilah* | Supererogatory | 17:79 |
| *Taṭawwaʿa khayran* | Voluntary good | 2:158, 2:184 |
| *Lā junāḥa* | Permission | 2:158, 2:198, 4:101 |
| *Khayrun lakum* | Recommended | 2:184, 2:280 |

**No consistent deontic scale:**

- Bare imperatives can be permissions: 5:2 *fa-ṣṭādū*; 62:10 *fa-ntashirū*; 2:187 *kulū wa-shrabū*.
- *Lā junāḥa* is used for Safa–Marwa (2:158), which is plausibly a rite.

The text gives **no general rule** for classifying an imperative. **Not every case can be classified from the text alone.**

### 12.7 Strongest evidence for internal completeness

1. 6:114 *al-kitāba mufaṣṣalā*; 16:89 *tibyānan li-kulli shayʾ*; 12:111 *tafṣīla kulli shayʾ*.
2. 6:145: the Prophet's own knowledge of prohibited food is limited to "what was revealed to me".
3. 66:1, 5:87, 7:32, 10:59, 16:116, 42:21: sustained hostility to prohibitions without God's authorisation.
4. 5:3: religion perfected.
5. 5:101: silence as pardon.
6. 45:6, 77:50: no *ḥadīth* after God's.
7. Detailed procedures exist exactly where human conflict is likely: inheritance, debt, divorce. That suggests deliberate selection of what to specify.

### 12.8 Most significant unresolved questions

1. **Presupposed referents.** The text commands acts it does not define (prayer form, Hajj months, the "known right"). Completeness of **guidance** (*hudā*) is distinguishable from completeness of **procedure**, and the Qur'an claims the former explicitly. Whether *tafṣīl kulli shayʾ* also claims the latter is **ambiguous**:
   - 12:111's *tafṣīl* follows the Joseph story.
   - 7:145 uses the same phrase of Moses' tablets, which are hardly a complete procedural code.
2. **Extra-textual revelation during the Prophet's lifetime** (2:143, 66:3, 8:7, 59:5). Its post-prophetic status is undetermined.
3. **Arithmetic and lexical gaps** in inheritance and divorce.
4. **The 2:184 / 2:185 relationship** and the general **abrogation** question (2:106) without named instances.
5. **The text's own identity and reading** (§2.2).

## 13. Original textual observations

**Caveat.** I cannot check whether these have been made before. Checking would require the literature this brief prohibits. "Original" therefore means **derived independently from the text here**, not "new to scholarship". Each observation is graded as a **finding** (well supported by the text) or a **hypothesis** (suggested, not established).

### 13.1 The Qur'an legislates the exception, not the norm — *Finding*

**Evidence.** In each ritual domain, detailed provisions cluster around deviations from a baseline that is never stated:

| Baseline (undescribed) | Detailed deviation |
|---|---|
| Normal prayer length | Shorten "from" it in fear (4:101) |
| Normal prayer posture | Walking/riding in danger (2:239); split congregation (4:102) |
| Washing with water | Dry ablution (4:43, 5:6) |
| Normal fast | Sick/traveller make-up; redemption (2:184) |
| Normal Hajj | Blocked pilgrim, ill pilgrim, *tamattuʿ*, hunting penalty (2:196, 5:95) |
| Normal oath-keeping | Expiation scale (5:89) |
| Normal marriage | *Ẓihār*, *īlāʾ*, *liʿān*, divorce stages (58, 2:226–232, 24:6–9) |

**Reasoning.** A text that defines the deviation in this much detail while omitting the norm is addressed to people who already **perform** the norm. Grammatically, phrases like *taqṣurū **min** al-ṣalāh* ("shorten *from* the prayer") need a known quantity.

**Limit.** This shows the norm was known to the original audience. It does not show *how* later generations are supposed to know it. A Qur'an-only advocate can say the norm was inherited Abrahamic practice. A traditionalist can say it was prophetic instruction. The text supports "presupposed" and is neutral on the source.

### 13.2 The *maʿlūm* delegations — *Finding*

**Evidence.** The adjective *maʿlūm* ("known") and the cognate *maʿdūd* ("counted") attach to exactly the quantities the Qur'an does not specify:

- *al-ḥajju ashhurun **maʿlūmāt*** (2:197): months not named.
- *fī ayyāmin **maʿlūmāt*** (22:28): days not named.
- *ḥaqqun **maʿlūm*** (70:24): amount not given. Compare 51:19, the same phrase **without** *maʿlūm*.
- *ayyāman **maʿdūdāt*** (2:184; 2:203): counted days.
- Related: *bi-l-**maʿrūf*** ("the recognised") as the standard for maintenance, treatment and settlement throughout 2:228–241 and 65:2–6.

**Reasoning.** The Qur'an is not *silent* on these quantities. It **marks them as known**. That is an explicit textual deferral to shared community knowledge. The 70:24 vs 51:19 contrast is especially telling: "known" was added where a determinate amount existed in practice.

**Limit.** The text does not say whether "known" knowledge is fixed (to be preserved) or contextual (re-determined per society). The *maʿrūf* passages lean contextual: "according to his means" (2:236; 65:7). The Hajj *maʿlūmāt* passages lean fixed.

### 13.3 Four references to divine communication not in the text — *Finding*, with a limited conclusion

**Evidence.**

- 2:143: an appointed earlier qiblah.
- 66:3: *nabbaʾaniya al-ʿalīm al-khabīr*.
- 8:7: a promise.
- 59:5: a permission.
- Supporting: 2:187 (prior restriction), 2:239 (*kamā ʿallamakum*), 48:27 and 8:43 (dreams), 42:51 (multiple modes of revelation).

**Reasoning.** The Qur'an refers retrospectively to divine communications whose content it does not contain. So "all revelation to Muhammad = the Qur'an" is **not a Qur'anic claim**. The Qur'an's own account contradicts it.

**Limit.**

- All four instances are situational and lifetime-bound.
- At least one (the earlier qiblah) was superseded.
- None is presented as standing law.
- So this establishes **existence**, not **continuing authority** or **preservation**.

### 13.4 *Bayyana* versus *katama* — *Finding* (lexical), *hypothesis* (application)

**Evidence.**

- 2:159: *yaktumūna… min baʿdi mā bayyannāhu li-l-nās*.
- 3:187: *la-tubayyinunnahu li-l-nāsi wa-lā taktumūnah*.
- 2:174 is related.
- In these verses *bayyana* is the **antonym of concealment**.
- 16:44: *li-tubayyina li-l-nāsi mā nuzzila ilayhim*.
- 75:19: *thumma inna ʿalaynā bayānah*.

**Reasoning.** Read with its Qur'anic antonym, 16:44's mandate can mean "to proclaim openly", not necessarily "to supplement with commentary". 75:19 assigns the *bayān* of the Qur'an to God.

**Limit.** *Bayyana* also means "explain" elsewhere (e.g. 2:187 *kadhālika yubayyinu Allāhu āyātihi*; 14:4). The "proclaim" reading is **available**, not **required**.

### 13.5 *Ḥikmah* is recited and is labelled on Qur'anic content — *Finding*

**Evidence.**

- 17:39 labels the preceding Qur'anic commands (17:22–38) as *ḥikmah* revealed to the Prophet.
- 33:34 has *ḥikmah* "recited" (*yutlā*) in the Prophet's houses.
- 2:231 treats "the Book and the wisdom" as one admonition (*yaʿiẓukum bihi*, singular pronoun).

**Reasoning.** The Qur'an provides at least one explicit instance of *ḥikmah* = Qur'anic text. So the pairing *al-kitāb wa-l-ḥikmah* does not by itself prove a second, non-Qur'anic corpus.

**Limit.** It does not prove that *ḥikmah* is **exclusively** Qur'anic either. "Wisdom" may include practical judgement.

### 13.6 The asymmetry of *uswah* — *Finding*

**Evidence.**

- *Uswah ḥasanah* occurs at 33:21 (Muhammad), 60:4 and 60:6 (Abraham).
- Abraham's example is specified in the Qur'an, including an **explicit exception** (60:4: not his promise to seek forgiveness for his father).
- Muhammad's example is unspecified in content. Its immediate context is endurance during the siege (33:9–27).

**Reasoning.** In the one case where the Qur'an specifies an *uswah*, it does so **textually** and corrects it **textually**. An analogy suggests that the Qur'an treats exemplary conduct as accessible through its own narrative.

**Limit.** The analogy is suggestive. The difference may be explained by audience: Muhammad's contemporaries could observe him directly and needed no textual specification. *Hypothesis-level as an argument for either side.*

### 13.7 Ritual words do not fix ritual bodies — *Finding*

**Evidence.**

- Prostration language describes:
  - entering a gate (2:58);
  - cosmic submission (13:15; 16:49; 22:18; 55:6);
  - honouring Joseph (12:100).
- Bowing language describes David "falling" (38:24).
- The order of the two verbs reverses between 3:43 and 22:26.

**Reasoning.** The words *rukūʿ* and *sujūd* cannot by themselves specify body positions or order. Only contextual markers determine the physical sense in prayer: "on their chins" (17:107), and "when they have prostrated, go behind" (4:102). The Qur'an can establish that prayer involves bowing and prostration. It cannot establish how many, in what order, or what each looks like. **This gap is lexical, not merely an omission.**

**Limit.** None significant. This is a firm negative result.

### 13.8 *Iqāmah* versus *ṣalāh* — *Hypothesis*

**Evidence.**

- The community is almost always commanded *aqīmū al-ṣalāh*, "establish/keep upright".
- The verb *ṣallā* ("pray") is used mainly for describing the act (4:102; 75:31; 87:15; 96:10; 108:2 *fa-ṣalli li-rabbika wa-nḥar*).
- *Aqāma* elsewhere means to maintain upright or uphold a structure or norm:
  - 18:77: *fa-aqāmahu*, a wall;
  - 42:13: *an aqīmū al-dīn*;
  - 55:9: *aqīmū al-wazn*, the balance;
  - 65:2: *aqīmū al-shahādah*.

**Reasoning.** The command may target the **maintenance of an institution** (regular communal prayer) rather than only individual performance. That would fit the Qur'an's presupposition of an existing rite.

**Limit.** 108:2 commands *ṣalli* directly. *Aqāma* + *ṣalāh* may simply be idiomatic. Classified as a **hypothesis**.

### 13.9 *Zakāh/ṣalāh* mirrors *tazakkā/ṣallā* — *Hypothesis*

**Evidence.** 87:14–15: *qad aflaḥa man tazakkā, wa-dhakara isma rabbihi fa-ṣallā*. Personal self-purification is paired with prayer, using the same roots as the institutional pair *zakāh / ṣalāh*. 92:18: *alladhī yuʾtī mālahu yatazakkā*, "who gives his wealth to purify himself". This joins giving wealth to self-purification.

**Reasoning.** *Zakāh* may be primarily a **purifying giving**, with its fiscal form secondary. That fits:

- its presence among earlier prophets (19:31, 19:55);
- its early Meccan occurrence (41:7);
- its lack of quantification;
- the fact that the 9:60 recipient list is assigned to *ṣadaqāt*, not to *zakāh*.

**Limit.** This does not disprove a fixed levy. 9:103 shows collection with a purification rationale. Classified as a **hypothesis** about emphasis.

### 13.10 The arithmetic of 2:233 + 46:15, and the arithmetic failure of 4:11–12 — *Finding*

**Evidence.**

- Thirty months for gestation and weaning (46:15), minus two years of nursing (2:233; 31:14), gives six months.
- The fixed fractions of 4:11–12 can sum to more than one.

**Reasoning.** The Qur'an invites cross-verse arithmetic in one place, where it yields a clean result. In another place, the same method yields an inconsistency the text does not resolve. A Qur'an-only jurisprudence would have to adopt a **reduction rule** that the Qur'an does not contain.

**Limit.** The inheritance gap applies only to certain heir combinations. It is real but bounded.

### 13.11 Food is the only domain with an explicit exhaustiveness clause — *Finding*

**Evidence.**

- 6:145 *lā ajidu fī mā ūḥiya ilayya muḥarraman… illā* ("I do not find in what was revealed to me anything forbidden… except"). Also 2:173 and 16:115, both with *innamā* ("only").
- No comparable clause exists for prayer, Hajj, or zakat.

**Reasoning.**

- In the dietary domain, the Qur'an explicitly claims completeness and explicitly ties the Prophet's prohibitive authority to revelation.
- Any additional food prohibitions would conflict with 6:145's wording.
- In ritual domains no such clause exists. So the Qur'an's completeness claims are **domain-variable**, not uniform.

**Limit.**

- 6:145 is in a polemical context: the polytheists' arbitrary food taboos (6:136–144).
- 7:157's *khabāʾith* leaves a residual undefined category.

### 13.12 The Qur'an's own outward referrals — *Finding*

**Evidence.**

- 2:199: *afīḍū min ḥaythu afāḍa al-nās*.
- 16:43 and 21:7: *fa-sʾalū ahla al-dhikr*.
- 10:94: ask those who read the earlier scripture.
- 16:123: follow Abraham's *millah*.
- 4:83: those who derive (*yastanbiṭūnahu*).
- 9:122: those who gain understanding (*yatafaqqahū*).

**Reasoning.** The Qur'an never presents itself as epistemically closed. It sends readers to practice, earlier scriptural communities, and qualified people. A strict "nothing but the text" methodology is **stricter than the Qur'an's own epistemology**.

**Limit.**

- None of these referrals names hadith or a prophetic-report corpus.
- They refer to (a) communal practice, (b) earlier scriptural communities, and (c) contemporaneous experts.
- So they undermine *textual isolationism*. They do not establish any *specific* external authority.

  - The definition of *qurūʾ* and *tafath*.
  - Inheritance over-subscription and the *kalālah* discrepancy.

- **The Prophet's life:** most biographical facts.
- **Occasions of revelation:** most of them.

### 14.4 What a Muslim could know about Muhammad

| Kind of knowledge | Extent |
|---|---|
| **Theological profile** | **Substantial**: mission, revelation, message, character, accountability, limits, mortality, finality |
| **Episodic history** | **Partial**: orphanhood, rejection, plots, emigration, Badr, Ḥunayn, an unnamed defeat, the Confederates, a pledge under the tree, treaty conflicts, household tensions, Zayd |
| **Biography** | **Minimal**: no parents, dates, age, names of wives and children, sequence, or death |

**Textual certainty:** the profile. **Inference:** the identification of unnamed battles and events. **Unresolved:** almost all chronology.

### 14.5 Procedural freedom of a Qur'an-only community

| Domain | Freedom |
|---|---|
| Ritual form | **Large** |
| Calendar and Hajj specifics | **Moderate** (constrained by the existing physical Hajj in practice) |
| Family law details | **Small to moderate** |
| Food | **Small** |
| Ethics | **Very small** |

The freedom is greatest exactly where the Qur'an marks content as "known" or presupposed.

### 14.6 Could a coherent, functional religious system result?

**Yes, with qualifications that are textual, not theological:**

1. **Coherent ethically and legally: yes.** Ethics and law are the Qur'an's most specified domains.
2. **Functional ritually: yes.** A community can pray, fast, give and make pilgrimage on Qur'anic terms.
3. **Not uniform.** Communities would differ on ritual form.
   - The Qur'an provides mechanisms to live with this: consultation, ease, silence-as-pardon, and God as final judge (5:48).
   - It provides no mechanism to eliminate it.
4. **Not fully self-contained.**
   - The text itself presupposes knowledge it does not contain (*maʿlūm*, *afāḍa al-nās*, the earlier qiblah, Abrahamic rites).
   - A Qur'an-only community therefore either:
     - invents conventions and labels them as such, or
     - accepts **some** inherited practice as a non-textual referent.
   - The first is coherent but historically discontinuous. The second abandons strict textual isolation without necessarily adopting hadith.

### 14.7 Which contemporary practices have clear Qur'anic foundations?

**Clear foundation:**

- The duty and times of prayer.
- Ablution and tayammum.
- Qiblah.
- Bowing and prostration.
- Friday prayer.
- The Ramadan fast and its boundaries.
- Zakat as an obligation.
- Recipients of *ṣadaqāt*.
- Hajj and its main rites: *ṭawāf*, Safa–Marwa, ʿArafāt, sacrifice, shaving.
- Most marriage, divorce and inheritance rules.
- Food prohibitions.
- Avoiding intoxicants.
- Modesty norms.
- Oath expiation.

**Require information beyond the text for their precise form:**

- Five fixed prayers (ambiguous in the text).
- Unit counts and the internal structure of prayer.
- Prayer formulas.
- The call to prayer.
- The form of the Friday sermon.
- Make-up and combining of prayers.
- Menstrual exemptions.
- The zakat rate and threshold.
- The end-of-Ramadan festival and its charity.
- The Hajj calendar.
- Stoning, Minā, Muzdalifah.
- *Iḥrām* garments.
- Circuit counts.
- The second festival.
- Specific slaughter technique.
- Theft thresholds.
- Any death penalty for *zinā*.
- Hair covering, as a matter of textual certainty.
- Most of the Prophet's biography.

**On the authority question itself:** the Qur'an firmly establishes the Messenger's authority beyond recitation **during his life**. It does not settle whether that authority continues through non-Qur'anic material **after** his death. Any position on that question rests on a premise the text does not supply.
