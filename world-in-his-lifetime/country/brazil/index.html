Brazil 570–632 CE

Diverse Amazonian and highland societies (Marajoara, Acre geoglyph builders, southern proto-Jê and others)

On Marajó Island, the Marajoara (c. 300–1350) lived on large earth mounds, built fishponds, and made elaborate pottery and burial urns. In Acre, people used huge geometric ditched enclosures (geoglyphs) as ceremonial gathering places within long-managed forest. In the southern highlands, proto-Jê groups of the Taquara-Itararé tradition, ancestors of the Kaingang and Xokleng, built pit-house villages and earthworks. No writing; dates come from excavation.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    Marajoara communities on Marajó Island live on raised mounds, manage fishponds and bury ancestors in decorated urns (c. 300–1350).
  2. c. 600
    Geometric ditched enclosures (geoglyphs) in Acre are built and used sporadically as ceremonial sites (c. 2000–650 years ago).
  3. c. 600
    Southern proto-Jê groups of the Taquara-Itararé tradition (c. 380 BC–17th century) live in pit-house villages in the southern highlands.
