Chile 570–632 CE

Atacama oasis communities linked to Tiwanaku; Bato and Llolleo horticulturalists in central Chile

In the north, the San Pedro de Atacama oases were drawn into Tiwanaku's economic and ideological sphere, probably through caravan trade rather than conquest; Tiwanaku-style goods appear mainly in burials. In central Chile, Bato and Llolleo groups of the Early Ceramic period lived in the Santiago basin; Llolleo numbers grew after about 700 with greater reliance on maize. Knowledge is archaeological only.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    San Pedro de Atacama burials include Tiwanaku-style textiles, drinking cups and hallucinogen paraphernalia, reflecting links with the Titicaca basin (c. 400–1000).
  2. c. 600
    Bato and Llolleo horticultural groups occupy the Santiago basin during the Early Ceramic period (c. 200 BC–AD 1300).
