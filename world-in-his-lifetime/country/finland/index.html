Finland 570–632 CE

Permanently settled communities in the south and west; hunter-fishers in the north (no state)

Archaeologists call this the Merovingian period (c. 550–800). Permanent settlement was concentrated in southern and western Finland, including Ostrobothnia, while the far north had no permanent settlement and was used by hunting and fishing groups, called Lapps (Sámi) in older scholarship. In Ostrobothnia, finds are poorer than in the preceding Migration Period and western contacts declined. Evidence is almost entirely archaeological.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    Ostrobothnian finds become fewer and poorer than in the Migration Period, reflecting weaker contacts with central Sweden.

Sources
