Bulgaria 570–632 CE

Eastern Roman Empire (Thrace and the lower Danube), under Avar and Slav pressure

This was Roman Thrace and the lower Danube frontier, close to Constantinople. Slavs who had besieged Sirmium and Singidunum in the 580s pushed into the Balkans, and by the end of the century much of present-day Bulgaria was Slavicized. Emperor Maurice campaigned constantly and forced the Avars back across the Danube by 602, but his order for troops to winter beyond the river provoked the mutiny that made Phocas emperor. Afterwards the frontier crumbled.

Timeline

  1. c. 585
    After the Avar and Slav sieges of Sirmium and Singidunum in the 580s, Slavs push deep into the Balkans.
  2. c. 595
    Maurice's armies campaign repeatedly in the Balkans, forcing the Avars back across the Danube by 602.
  3. 602
    The Danube army mutinies over wintering north of the river; Phocas marches on Constantinople and Maurice is killed.
  4. 626
    The Avars and their Slav allies, coordinating with a Persian army, besiege Constantinople and fail.
