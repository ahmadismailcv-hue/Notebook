India 570–632 CE

Harsha of Thanesar and Kannauj (north), Chalukyas of Badami (Deccan), Pallavas of Kanchi (southeast), Valabhi kingdom (west)

After the Gupta collapse, regional dynasties competed. Harsha (r. 606–647) ruled from Thanesar and later Kannauj; his court poet Bana wrote the Harshacharita. The Chalukya king Pulakeshin II (r. 609–642) defeated him on the Narmada, which became Harsha's southern frontier. Chalukyas and Pallavas, under Mahendravarman I (c. 600–630), fought over Vengi. Xuanzang reached India around 630 and presided over a great Buddhist assembly held by Harsha.

Timeline

  1. c. 578
    Shrines to Shiva and Vishnu are built at Badami, base of the Early Chalukya dynasty.
  2. 606
    Harsha, aged 16, becomes king of Thanesar after Shashanka of Gauda kills his elder brother Rajyavardhana.
  3. 609
    Pulakeshin II begins his reign as Chalukya king of Badami, ruling until 642.
  4. c. 630
    Pulakeshin II defeats Harsha on the Narmada, which becomes the southern limit of Harsha's empire (date disputed; one source gives 630–634).
