Peru 570–632 CE

Late Moche polities (Pampa Grande), the expanding Wari state, late Nazca, Recuay; Tiwanaku colonies in Moquegua

A time of upheaval. Severe droughts and El Niño floods strained the north-coast Moche; new inland centres such as Pampa Grande rose as the old southern capital declined. In the highlands, the Wari state was expanding from its capital near Ayacucho. Nazca culture was in its late phase on the south coast and Recuay persisted in the north highlands. Tiwanaku colonists from the Titicaca basin farmed the Moquegua valley.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    Droughts and El Niño floods (dated c. 560–600 by some, c. 630 by others) strain the Moche; the southern capital at Moche is largely abandoned.
  2. c. 600
    Pampa Grande (c. 550–700), with the huge Huaca Fortaleza platform, becomes the largest Moche city, in the Lambayeque valley.
  3. c. 600
    Wari, north of modern Ayacucho, is a prosperous metropolis and capital of a rapidly expanding state, with specialized craft compounds.
  4. c. 600
    Tiwanaku colonists from the altiplano farm the Moquegua valley, beginning with Omo-style settlements.
  5. c. 600
    Late Nazca culture (c. 100–700) continues on the south coast; Nasca styles shape Wari's Chakipampa ceramics.
