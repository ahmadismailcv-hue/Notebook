Belarus 570–632 CE

Baltic-speaking peoples, with Slavs pushing in (no state)

Archaeology shows the Bancer-Tushemlya culture (6th–8th centuries) on the upper Dnieper, upper Neman and Daugava, which most researchers attribute to Baltic tribes. People farmed and kept livestock, lived in open settlements, used hillforts as refuges, built small round sanctuaries, and cremated their dead, burying the remains under mounds. The culture declined under intensive Slavic expansion northward.
