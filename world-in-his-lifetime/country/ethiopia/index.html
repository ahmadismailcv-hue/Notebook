Ethiopia 570–632 CE

Kingdom of Aksum (ruler known in Islamic tradition as al-Najashi, Ashama)

Aksum was a Christian kingdom, one of the first to adopt Christianity in the 4th century, and a link between African, Mediterranean and Asian trade. Its rule in Yemen ended when Sasanian Persia expelled Ethiopian forces in the later 6th century. According to Islamic sources, its ruler, the Negus Ashama (al-Najashi), sheltered Muslim emigrants from Arabia, among them Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, from c. 615.

Connection to Arabia According to Islamic sources, the Negus Ashama (al-Najashi) sheltered Muslim emigrants from c. 615, including Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, and Muhammad ﷺ led the funeral prayer for him when he died.

Timeline

  1. c. 615
    First Hijra: Muslim emigrants from Arabia are granted protection by the Christian Aksumite kingdom.
  2. c. 628
    Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and the remaining emigrants in Ethiopia rejoin Muhammad ﷺ at the time of the conquest of Khaybar, per Sahih al-Bukhari.
  3. c. 630
    The Negus Ashama dies (date uncertain); per Sahih al-Bukhari, Muhammad ﷺ announced his death and led the funeral prayer for him.
