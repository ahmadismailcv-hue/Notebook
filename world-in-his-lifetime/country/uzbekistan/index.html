Uzbekistan 570–632 CE

Sogdian city-states (Samarkand, Bukhara) under Western Turkic overlordship

Sogdiana's city-states lay north of the Oxus under Turkic overlordship; Samarkand was a major trading emporium, and Bukhara's rulers deferred to its ruler. Sogdian merchants dominated Silk Road trade and gave the Turks their first chancellery language. Samarkand hosted Zoroastrians, Christians, Buddhists and Manichaeans, and Buddhist monasteries stood at Termez in the south.

Timeline

  1. c. 568
    Sogdian merchants persuade the Turkic khagan to open contact with Byzantium to sell Chinese silk (between 568 and 575).
  2. c. 628
    Xuanzang passes through Samarkand and Termez, where he notes about a dozen Buddhist monasteries and some 1,000 monks.
