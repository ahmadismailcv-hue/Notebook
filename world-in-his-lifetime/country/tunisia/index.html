Byzantine Exarchate of Africa (capital Carthage)

Carthage was the centre of Byzantine Africa. In the late 6th century Emperor Maurice (r. 582-602) made it an exarchate whose governor held military and civil power. In 610 Heraclius, son of the governor of Carthage of the same name, led a fleet to Constantinople, where Phocas was overthrown and Heraclius proclaimed emperor. Amid later defeats he considered moving his capital to Carthage.

Timeline

  1. c. 590
    Emperor Maurice (r. 582-602) establishes the exarchate of Carthage, its exarch holding military and civil power; the exact date is uncertain.
  2. 610
    Heraclius, son of the governor of Carthage, leads a fleet to Constantinople; Phocas is overthrown and dies on 5 October, and Heraclius becomes emperor.
  3. c. 619
    After heavy losses to Persia, Heraclius considers moving his capital to Carthage but is persuaded to stay by Bishop Sergios of Constantinople and the people.
