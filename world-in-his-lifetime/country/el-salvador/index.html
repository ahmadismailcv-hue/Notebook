El Salvador 570–632 CE

Maya and other farming communities (e.g., San Andrés, Joya de Cerén)

The region had recovered from the huge Tierra Blanca Joven eruption of Ilopango, now dated to 431 ± 2 CE (it was once linked to c. 540). Ash-covered land was resettled once it weathered into fertile soil. San Andrés was reoccupied and urbanised, becoming the centre of the Zapotitán valley. Around 600 the farming village of Joya de Cerén was buried by the Loma Caldera eruption, preserving houses, fields and tools.

Timeline

  1. c. 575
    San Andrés, reoccupied after the Ilopango eruption (431 CE), grows into the political, economic and religious centre of the Zapotitán valley.
  2. c. 600
    Loma Caldera erupts and buries Joya de Cerén, preserving buildings, maize fields and household goods (dated c. 600; also given as 590 ± 90).
