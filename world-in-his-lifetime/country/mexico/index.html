Mexico 570–632 CE

Teotihuacan in decline; Maya kingdoms such as Palenque and the Kaanul (Snake) kingdom

Teotihuacan, once the largest city in the Americas, had its major buildings deliberately burned around 600 and lost its regional dominance, though people kept living there. In the Maya south, the Kaanul (Snake) kingdom, then based at Dzibanché, attacked Palenque in 599 and again in 611. Pakal, born 603, became king of Palenque aged twelve in 615. In the 630s the Kaanul royal seat shifted to Calakmul.

Timeline

  1. 599
    Kaanul (Snake) forces from Dzibanché attack Palenque; the attack is credited to the Kaanul king Sky Witness, though this placement is chronologically puzzling.
  2. c. 600
    Teotihuacan's major buildings are deliberately burned and sculptures smashed; the city stays inhabited but loses its regional dominance.
  3. 603
    K'inich Janaab Pakal, the future king of Palenque, is born.
  4. 611
    The Kaanul king Scroll Serpent attacks Palenque, the second Kaanul assault there, as recorded in Palenque's Temple of the Inscriptions.
  5. 612
    Muwaan Mat becomes ruler of Palenque after the death of Aj Ne' Yohl Mat.
  6. 615
    Pakal, aged twelve, becomes king of Palenque; he rules until 683.
  7. 636
    After a Kaanul civil war (630–640), Yuknoom Ch'een II accedes as founder of a reconstituted Kaanul kingdom whose capital has shifted from Dzibanché to Calakmul.
