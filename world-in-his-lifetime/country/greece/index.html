Greece 570–632 CE

Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire, with Slav settlement inland

Greece was Greek-speaking, Christian Roman territory ruled from Constantinople. From the late 6th century many Slavs entered it, and together with the Avar wars they disrupted the economy: building of churches and secular works almost ceased, and newcomers reached deep into the Peloponnese. Inland, Slavic chieftaincies (sklaviniai) dominated many districts, while fortified cities with sea access, such as Thessalonica and Corinth, stayed in imperial hands.

Timeline

  1. 586
    Avars and Slavs besiege Thessalonica; a local miracle collection credits Saint Demetrius with saving the city.
  2. 610
    Heraclius overthrows Phocas and becomes emperor.
  3. c. 620
    Slav tribes attack Thessalonica (c. 620), and in 622 the Avars besiege it, according to the Miracles of Saint Demetrius.
  4. c. 620
    Newcomers settle deep in the Peloponnese; the later, controversial Chronicle of Monemvasia describes Greek flight (dates disputed).
