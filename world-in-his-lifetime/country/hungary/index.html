Hungary 570–632 CE

Avar Khaganate (heartland in the Carpathian Basin)

From 567–568 the Hungarian plain was the centre of the Avar Khaganate, a steppe power whose ruling elite was largely of East-Central Asian origin and which dominated Slavic peoples. The Avars took Sirmium in 582 and, between periods of Byzantine subsidies and tribute, pressed the Balkans until their failed siege of Constantinople in 626, after which their power waned. Rich 7th-century elite graves of the Kunbábony group reflect their wealth.

Timeline

  1. 582
    The Avars capture the Balkan fortress of Sirmium.
  2. c. 604
    Phocas concludes a peace with the Avars, freeing troops for the Persian war.
  3. 623
    Heraclius secures peace with the Avars by promising massive tribute payments.
  4. 626
    An Avar host besieges Constantinople with Slav allies, coordinated with a Persian army; the assault fails.
