Japan 570–632 CE

Yamato court (Asuka period) under Empress Suiko, then Emperor Jomei

The Yamato court ruled from Asuka. From 587 the Soga clan dominated government. Empress Suiko (r. 592–628) reigned with Prince Shotoku as regent. Buddhism was promoted and temples such as Shitennoji, Hokoji and Horyuji were built, while teachers and scholars from Baekje in Korea brought Chinese learning such as Confucian texts. Official embassies to Sui China began about 607.

Timeline

  1. 587
    The Soga clan establishes control of the government, holding it until 645; the rival Mononobe clan had resisted Buddhism.
  2. 594
    Prince Shotoku becomes regent for Empress Suiko, Japan's first empress, and governs on her behalf until his death in 622.
  3. 604
    The Seventeen Article Constitution, credited to Prince Shotoku, sets out Buddhist and Confucian principles of government.
  4. c. 607
    Horyuji temple is completed, and Japan sends the first of many official embassies to the Sui court.
  5. 622
    Death of Prince Shotoku.
  6. 629
    Jomei becomes emperor, reigning until 641.
