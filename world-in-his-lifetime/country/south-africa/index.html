South Africa 570–632 CE

Iron-using farmers in the east; herders and foragers in the west

Iron-using farmers, directly ancestral to the Bantu-speaking peoples who form South Africa's majority today, had arrived from about 200 CE. Their sites in the east show pottery, metal-working, cultivation and livestock. The Lydenburg Heads, the earliest sculpture found in southern Africa, date to c. 500-700 CE. Herders held an enduring presence in the drier west.
