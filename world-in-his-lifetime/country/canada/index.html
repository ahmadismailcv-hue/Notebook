Canada 570–632 CE

Hunter-gatherer and fishing peoples (Dorset Paleo-Inuit in the eastern Arctic, Plains bison hunters, Northwest Coast societies)

No states or writing; everything known comes from archaeology. The eastern Arctic was home to the Dorset Paleo-Inuit, then in their Late Dorset period; the early Inuit (Thule) arrived only centuries later. On the northern Plains, bison hunters had used the bow and arrow since about 200 CE. Northwest Coast peoples lived in permanent settlements built on salmon and cedar, with considerable wealth and political complexity.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    Late Dorset period (from c. 500): Dorset exchange networks span the eastern Arctic, though they have left Newfoundland and much of southern Labrador.
  2. c. 600
    On the northern Plains, bison hunting with the bow and arrow, associated with the Avonlea people since about 200 CE, continues.
  3. c. 600
    Northwest Coast peoples live in permanent settlements supported by salmon runs and cedar, without farming.
