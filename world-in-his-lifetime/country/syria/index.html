Syria 570–632 CE

Byzantine Empire, with Ghassanid Arab allies; Persian occupation c. 611–628

Syria was a rich Byzantine land of cities such as Antioch and Damascus, with Ghassanid Arab allies guarding the desert side; Meccan merchants traded here. Persian armies took Antioch about 610–612 and occupied Syria after defeating Heraclius in 613, holding it until Kavad II returned Persia's conquests in 628.

Connection to Arabia Meccan caravans traded in Syria; tradition says the young Muhammad ﷺ, travelling there with his uncle, was recognized as a future prophet by a Christian monk.

Timeline

  1. c. 611
    Persian armies under Shahrbaraz take Antioch (date disputed, 610–612).
  2. 613
    The Persians defeat a Byzantine army under Heraclius and occupy Syria, including Damascus.
  3. 628
    Kavad II returns his father's conquests, and Byzantine rule over Syria is restored.
  4. c. 637
    Antioch falls to the Arab Muslim armies (637–638).
