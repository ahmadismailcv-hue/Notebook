Yemen 570–632 CE

Former Himyarite kingdom under Ethiopian (Aksumite) rule, then a Sasanian Persian dependency from c. 570

After Ethiopia intervened in 525, south Arabia became an Ethiopian protectorate under Abraha and his son Masruq. About 570 a Persian force under Vahriz, backing Sayf ibn Dhi Yazan, killed Masruq and took Sanaa. Persian governors then ruled, and the descendants of Persian soldiers and Arab mothers were called the Abna. The last governor, Badhan, accepted Islam, recorded under 10 AH (631).

Connection to Arabia Yemen's Persian governor Badhan and other Abna leaders accepted Islam in the lifetime of Muhammad ﷺ, who confirmed the governor as his agent in Yemen.

Timeline

  1. c. 570
    According to Islamic tradition, Abraha of South Arabia tries to conquer Mecca in the year Muhammad ﷺ is born.
  2. c. 570
    A Persian force under Vahriz defeats and kills Abraha's son Masruq, ending Ethiopian rule (c. 570; some sources date Masruq's death to 575–576 or 582).
  3. 631
    Badhan, Persian governor of Yemen, and other Abna leaders accept Islam, recorded under 10 AH (631).
  4. 632
    A Yemeni rising led by al-Aswad, who claimed prophetic powers, expels agents of Muhammad ﷺ (March 632); al-Aswad is later killed.
