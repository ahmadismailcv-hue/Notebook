Bolivia 570–632 CE

Tiwanaku (Lake Titicaca basin); Casarabe culture in the Llanos de Mojos

Tiwanaku, near the south shore of Lake Titicaca, was in its apogee (c. 500–900) as a ceremonial and political centre with monuments such as the Akapana, Kalasasaya and Pumapunku. Raised-field farming and lake resources supported it, and its influence reached colonies in Moquegua and Cochabamba and, through caravan trade, distant oases. In the Amazonian lowlands, the Casarabe culture (c. 500–1400) built platform mounds, causeways and canals.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    A major construction programme begins at Tiwanaku, including the Akapana pyramid with seven stone-walled platforms and an expanded drainage system.
  2. c. 600
    Monumental human-form stone sculptures, including the Bennett Stela, are erected at Tiwanaku.
  3. c. 600
    The Casarabe culture (c. 500–1400) in the Llanos de Mojos develops settlements that later include platform mounds, raised causeways and canals.
