Spain 570–632 CE

Visigothic kingdom of Toledo (with Byzantine territory in the southeast until the 620s)

The Visigoths ruled from Toledo. Leovigild (568–586) conquered the Suebi, and his son Reccared (586–601) abandoned Arianism for Catholicism, removing a main obstacle between Goths and Hispano-Romans. Isidore, bishop of Seville from about 600 to 636, wrote the encyclopaedic Etymologies. Between about 612 and 616 King Sisebut decreed that all Jews must convert, and Swinthila (621–631) took the last Byzantine fortresses.

Timeline

  1. 585
    Leovigild conquers the Suebic kingdom in the northwest.
  2. 589
    The Visigoths formally convert from Arian to Catholic Christianity under Reccared.
  3. c. 600
    Isidore becomes bishop of Seville (c. 600); he later writes the Etymologies.
  4. c. 612
    Between about 612 and 616 King Sisebut decrees that all Jews of the kingdom must convert to Christianity.
  5. c. 625
    Swinthila (621–631) conquers the last Byzantine fortresses in Spain (exact date uncertain).
