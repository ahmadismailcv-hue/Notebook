Bangladesh 570–632 CE

Gauda kingdom of Shashanka (north and west); Samatata (east)

Northern Bangladesh lay in Gauda, whose king Shashanka, ruling from Karnasuvarna, held Magadha and lands in Odisha and fought the Maukharis and Harsha's family. Pundranagara (Mahasthangarh), founded around the 4th–3rd century BCE, was long an administrative centre with Buddhist monasteries and Brahmanical temples. Samatata in the east was famed as a Mahayana Buddhist centre, as Xuanzang's travel account attests.

Timeline

  1. c. 606
    Shashanka is blamed for killing Rajyavardhana, Harsha's brother; Harsha allies with Bhaskaravarman of Kamarupa against him.
  2. c. 635
    Xuanzang, travelling in India from about 630, records the fame of Samatata as a Mahayana Buddhist centre.
  3. 637
    Shashanka dies after a long reign; only now does Harsha gain direct control of the Gauda lands.
