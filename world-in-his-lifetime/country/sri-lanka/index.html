Sri Lanka 570–632 CE

Anuradhapura kingdom (Sinhalese kings recorded in the Culavamsa)

Sinhalese Buddhist kings ruled from Anuradhapura; the Culavamsa chronicle records them, but its dates are approximate and must be checked against inscriptions. Aggabodhi I and his nephew Aggabodhi II are remembered for irrigation works. The 6th–8th centuries were otherwise marked by murder, revolt and civil war, and from the 7th century South Indian powers and Tamil mercenaries grew more involved. The island lay on Indian Ocean trade routes.

Timeline

  1. c. 590
    Reign of Aggabodhi I (6th–7th century; exact dates uncertain), who restores Mahasena's channel to Minneriya and builds the Kurunduvewa tank.
  2. c. 609
    Under Aggabodhi II, credited with fourteen tanks, a fleeing Kalinga king takes refuge in Lanka and becomes a monk, possibly c. 609.
  3. c. 630
    From the 7th century South Indian powers grow more involved in island politics, with Tamil mercenaries in and around the capital.
