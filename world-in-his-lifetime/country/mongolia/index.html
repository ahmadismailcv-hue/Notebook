Mongolia 570–632 CE

Turkic (Kök Türk) Khaganate, from 583 its eastern branch

Mongolia was the heartland of the Turkic khaganate founded in 552, which split into eastern and western halves in 583. Its nomadic rulers, whose economy rested on herding, first wrote in Sogdian. The Eastern Turks were strongest under Shibi Qaghan (609–619), but after disasters in 627 and revolts led by the Xueyantuo, the Tang general Li Jing destroyed the khaganate in 630.

Timeline

  1. c. 581
    The Bugut stele, written in Sogdian, is raised for a dead khagan; it is the oldest surviving inscription on the history of the Turkic rulers.
  2. 583
    Rivalries within the ruling dynasty split the Turkic khaganate into eastern and western halves.
  3. 615
    Shibi Qaghan besieges the Sui emperor Yangdi at Yanmen, triggering rebellions across China.
  4. 626
    Illig Qaghan advances to the Tang capital Chang'an; the new emperor Taizong has to bribe the Turks to withdraw.
  5. 627
    Heavy snowfall kills the Turks' herds; famine and heavy taxes spark revolts by vassal tribes, which unite under the Xueyantuo.
  6. 630
    Tang general Li Jing destroys the Eastern Turkic khaganate in surprise raids; his subordinate Zhang Baoxiang captures Illig Qaghan.
