South Korea 570–632 CE

Silla (southeast) and Baekje (southwest), two of Korea's Three Kingdoms

The south was divided between Silla, ruled from Geumseong (Gyeongju), which had absorbed the Gaya confederation by 562, and Baekje in the southwest, ruled from Sabi (Buyeo) since 538. They had been enemies since Silla seized the lower Han River valley in the 550s. Both had made Buddhism a state religion; Baekje exported much of its culture to Japan. Jinpyeong of Silla (r. 579–632) was succeeded by his daughter Seondeok.

Timeline

  1. 579
    King Jinpyeong begins a long reign in Silla.
  2. 600
    King Mu takes the throne of Baekje, reigning until 641.
  3. 632
    Seondeok succeeds her father Jinpyeong, becoming the first female sovereign in ancient Korea.
