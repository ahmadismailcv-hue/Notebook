United States of America 570–632 CE

Diverse Native American societies (Late Woodland, Basketmaker III Ancestral Puebloans, Hohokam, Weeden Island and others)

No states, cities or writing. In the East, Late Woodland peoples (c. 600–900) built fewer burial mounds and long-distance trade declined, but the bow and arrow and new crop varieties spread and sites multiplied. On the Colorado Plateau, Basketmaker III Ancestral Puebloans farmed and lived in pithouse villages. Hohokam farmers lived along the Salt and Gila rivers. Weeden Island villages in north Florida and Georgia built mounds.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    Late Woodland period begins in the Southeast: Middle Woodland mound building and long-distance trade decline, while settlements become more numerous.
  2. c. 600
    The bow and arrow spreads in the East and replaces the atlatl among Ancestral Puebloans; new varieties of maize, beans and squash gain importance.
  3. c. 600
    Basketmaker III Ancestral Puebloans (settled at Mesa Verde from c. 550) live in pithouse villages, farm corn, beans and squash, and make pottery.
  4. c. 600
    Hohokam farming villages occupy the Phoenix Basin along the Salt and Gila rivers, a culture famed for extensive irrigation canals.
  5. c. 600
    Weeden Island I culture (c. 250–700) in north Florida and south Georgia: villages with mounds, charnel houses and elaborate ceremonial pottery.
