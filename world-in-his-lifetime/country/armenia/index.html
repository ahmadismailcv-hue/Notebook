Armenia 570–632 CE

Divided between the Byzantine Empire and Sasanian Persia (Persarmenia); Armenian noble houses

Armenia was ruled by noble families such as the Bagratunis and Mamikonians under two empires. In 591 Khosrow II ceded western Persian Armenia to Byzantium, but Persia recovered it after 603. A council at Dvin in 596 rejected Chalcedon. Heraclius campaigned through Armenia from 623, and his victories enabled him to insist on the frontier of 591.

Timeline

  1. 591
    Khosrow II cedes western Persian Armenia, up to the river Azat, to Byzantium in return for help in regaining his throne.
  2. 596
    A church council at Dvin, sponsored by Sembat Bagratuni, rejects the doctrines of the Council of Chalcedon.
  3. c. 607
    Persian forces take Karin (modern Erzurum) as they reconquer Byzantine Armenia (607–608).
  4. c. 623
    Heraclius campaigns through Armenia into Atropatene against Persian forces (623–624).
  5. c. 628
    After Heraclius's victories, Persia consents to restore the Armenian frontier fixed in 591.
