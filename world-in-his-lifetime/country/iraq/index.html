Iraq 570–632 CE

Sasanian Empire (capital Ctesiphon), with the Lakhmid Arab client kingdom at al-Hira until 602; Kurdish tribes in the northern mountains

Mesopotamia, with the capital Ctesiphon, was the most important part of the Sasanian Empire. Khosrow II ended the Lakhmid kingdom of al-Hira in 602, weakening the desert frontier. In the northern mountains, the edge of the Zagros, lived Kurdish tribes who herded sheep and goats. By the end of the Sasanian period the Kurds were a major people of the Zagros; the name Kurd is securely attested from the 7th century, when they converted to Islam.

Connection to Arabia The end of the Lakhmids and the Arab victory at Dhu Qar weakened Persia's Arabian frontier, which Muslim armies crossed in 633.

Timeline

  1. 602
    Khosrow II deposes and kills al-Nu'man III, ending the Lakhmid kingdom of al-Hira.
  2. c. 604
    Arab tribes led by the Bakr ibn Wa'il defeat a Persian force at Dhu Qar near Kufa; the date is disputed, c. 604–613.
  3. 627
    Heraclius defeats the Persian army near Nineveh on 12 December and advances toward Ctesiphon.
  4. 628
    Khosrow II is overthrown and executed; his son Kavad II makes peace with Byzantium and dies later that year.
  5. c. 637
    Arab Muslim armies win at al-Qadisiyya and take Ctesiphon (dates disputed, c. 636–637).
