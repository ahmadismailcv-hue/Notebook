Afghanistan 570–632 CE

Post-Hephthalite principalities and Western Turkic rulers in Tokharistan; Sasanian frontier near Herat

After the Western Turks and Sasanians destroyed the Hephthalites around 560–566, the land was divided. After 579, battles with the Western Turks cost the Sasanians control of Tokharistan (Bactria). Buddhism flourished: Balkh had about 100 monasteries, and the colossal Bamiyan Buddhas, dated to the 6th–7th centuries, stood in the central mountains. Xuanzang described these Buddhist lands around 628–632.

Timeline

  1. c. 579
    After 579, battles with the Western Turks over former Hephthalite lands cost the Sasanians control of Tokharistan (Bactria).
  2. 588
    Turk-backed Hephthalite forces reach Badghis and Herat; the Sasanian general Bahram Chobin defeats the Western Turks and takes Balkh.
  3. c. 600
    The colossal Bamiyan Buddhas belong to this era; sources date them to the 6th–7th centuries (dating debated).
  4. c. 630
    Xuanzang describes Buddhist Kapisa, Bamiyan and Tokharistan (c. 628–632); his account is the earliest text describing the Bamiyan Buddhas.
