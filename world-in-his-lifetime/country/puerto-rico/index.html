Puerto Rico 570–632 CE

Village societies in transition from Saladoid to Ostionoid

Puerto Rico had been farmed for about a thousand years by Saladoid peoples from South America, who lived in villages around central plazas and made fine white-on-red pottery. Around 600, archaeologists see major social reorganisation and a shift to the Ostionoid tradition, named after the Ostiones site in western Puerto Rico, with plainer red-slipped pottery. Ball courts and plazas belong to this Ostionoid era and later Taíno society.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    The Saladoid tradition gives way to the Ostionoid; pottery decoration simplifies and leadership and social status are reorganised.
  2. c. 620
    Ball court and plaza sites begin in the Early Ostionoid period (c. 600–1200); centres such as Tibes develop over the following centuries.
