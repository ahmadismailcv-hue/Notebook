Tajikistan 570–632 CE

Sogdian towns (Panjikant) in the north; Tokharistan principalities in the south under Western Turkic power

The north lay in Sogdiana, where Panjikant was a prosperous town of merchants and landowners; its two temples and many houses were decorated with murals of gods, royal feasts and epic heroes. The south belonged to Tokharistan, a hub of trade routes once dominated by the Hephthalites and later under Western Turkic control. Buddhist monasteries stood in northern Tokharistan.

Timeline

  1. c. 565
    The Western Turks and Sasanians destroy the Hephthalites (dated 558–561 or 563–566); the Turks take the lands north of the Oxus.
  2. c. 600
    Panjikant flourishes; its temples and houses bear murals of gods, feasts, hunts and heroes such as Rostam.
  3. c. 628
    Xuanzang, writing under the year 628, records Buddhist monasteries in northern Tokharistan.
