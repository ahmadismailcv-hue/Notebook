Madagascar 570–632 CE

Early settlers (Austronesian arrival dates debated)

The Malagasy language is related to Ma'anyan of southern Borneo, and Malagasy people have both Southeast Asian and African ancestry. When Austronesian seafarers arrived is debated: estimates range from the middle of the first millennium CE to the 7th-8th centuries, and Southeast Asian crops found at early sites give the first archaeological evidence of their presence.

Timeline

  1. c. 630
    Austronesian settlers may be reaching Madagascar, estimated on linguistic grounds at about the 7th-8th centuries; dates remain debated.

Sources

  • Crowther et al., Ancient crops provide first archaeological signature of the westward Austronesian expansion (2016), PNAS
  • Kusuma et al., Contrasting Linguistic and Genetic Origins of the Asian Source Populations of Malagasy (2016), Scientific Reports (Nature)
