Colombia 570–632 CE

Regional chiefdom societies (San Agustín, Tierradentro, San Jorge floodplain and others)

Colombia had chiefdom societies but no empire or writing. Around San Agustín in the upper Magdalena, the Regional Classic period (c. 1–900) saw elite burial mounds with stone tombs and large carved statues. At Tierradentro, monumental painted shaft-and-chamber tombs (hypogea) were cut into volcanic tuff from about 600. In the San Jorge floodplain of the north, ridges and canals managed floodwaters, with the region at its height c. 400–1000.

Timeline

  1. c. 600
    San Agustín's Regional Classic period (c. 1–900) continues, with burial mounds over stone tombs guarded by large carved statues.
  2. c. 600
    At Tierradentro, communities begin cutting decorated underground tomb chambers (hypogea) into volcanic tuff (c. 600–900).
  3. c. 600
    In the San Jorge floodplain, ridges and drainage canals manage seasonal floods; the region is at its height c. 5th–10th centuries.
