China 570–632 CE

Northern Zhou, then Sui (581–618) and Tang (618–); Tibetan kingdom of Songtsen Gampo in the west

Yang Jian (Emperor Wen) founded the Sui in 581 and reunified China by conquering the south in 589. His son Yangdi (r. 604–618) built the Grand Canal but exhausted the realm with failed wars against Goguryeo. Yangdi was assassinated in 618 and Li Yuan founded the Tang. His son Taizong seized power in 626, and in 630 Tang forces destroyed the Eastern Turkic khaganate. Songtsen Gampo expanded Tibet.

Timeline

  1. 589
    Sui armies capture Nanjing and conquer the south, reunifying China under Emperor Wen after three centuries of division.
  2. 604
    Yangdi becomes emperor; his reign sees the building of the Grand Canal linking the Yangtze and Yellow rivers.
  3. 612
    Yangdi personally leads a huge invasion of Goguryeo, which is crushed at the Salsu River; further campaigns in 613 and 614 also fail.
  4. 615
    The Eastern Turkic ruler Shibi Qaghan besieges Yangdi at Yanmen, fuelling the rebellions that bring down the Sui.
  5. 618
    Yangdi is assassinated; Li Yuan, Duke of Tang, founds the Tang dynasty and reigns as Gaozu.
  6. 626
    Li Shimin kills his brothers, including the heir Li Jiancheng, in a coup; Gaozu abdicates and Li Shimin reigns as Taizong.
  7. 629
    Songtsen Gampo is crowned king of Tibet; he later extends his rule to Nepal and parts of India and China.
  8. 630
    Tang general Li Jing destroys the Eastern Turkic khaganate in surprise raids; his subordinate Zhang Baoxiang captures the khagan Illig.
