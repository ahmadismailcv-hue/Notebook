The life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Following Ibn Isḥāq as edited by Ibn Hishām (tr. A. Guillaume, The Life of Muhammad ﷺ, Oxford University Press, 1955) and Ṣafī al-Raḥmān al-Mubārakpūrī, Ar-Raḥīq al-Makhtūm (The Sealed Nectar), Darussalam. Where the two differ, both are given.

  1. c. 570
    Muhammad ﷺ is born in Mecca in the Year of the Elephant, after his father Abdullah has died. Ibn Ishaq: 12 Rabi' al-Awwal; Sealed Nectar: 9 Rabi' al-Awwal, April 571.53 years before the Hijra · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), p. 69; Sealed Nectar, 'His Birth', p. 56; Britannica, 'Muhammad' (c. 570)
  2. c. 571
    As an infant he is entrusted to the wet nurse Halima bint Abi Dhu'ayb of Banu Sa'd and raised among the desert Bedouin.Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'His birth and fostermother', pp. 69-73; Sealed Nectar, 'Babyhood', p. 57
  3. c. 576
    At about six his mother Amina dies at al-Abwa' returning from Yathrib. His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib takes him in; at eight he passes to his uncle Abu Talib.Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), pp. 73, 79; Sealed Nectar, 'Back to his passionate Mother' and 'To His compassionate Grandfather', p. 59
  4. c. 595
    At about 25, Muhammad ﷺ marries Khadija bint Khuwaylid, a respected merchant of Mecca. She is his only wife until her death.Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), p. 82 and Ibn Hisham's note 126; Sealed Nectar, 'His Marriage to Khadijah', p. 62
  5. c. 605
    When he is 35, Quraysh rebuild the Ka'ba. A dispute over replacing the Black Stone is settled by his arbitration, the clans lifting it together on a cloak.Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'Rebuilding of the Ka'ba', p. 84; Sealed Nectar, 'Rebuilding Al-Ka'bah and the Arbitration Issue', p. 63
  6. 610
    At about 40, the first revelation comes to Muhammad ﷺ in the cave of Hira' in Ramadan. Sealed Nectar dates it to Monday 21 Ramadan, 10 August 610.13 years before the Hijra · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), pp. 104-105; Sealed Nectar, 'In the Shade of the Message and Prophethood', p. 67
  7. c. 613
    After about three years of private calling, he is commanded to preach openly, beginning with his near kin and the address from Mount al-Safa.About 10 years before the Hijra · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), p. 117; Sealed Nectar, 'Three years of Secret Call' and 'The Second Phase: Open Preaching', pp. 77-82
  8. 615
    Facing persecution, a group of twelve men and four women, including Uthman and Ruqayya, migrates to Abyssinia under the Negus, in Rajab.5th year of prophethood · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'The first emigrants to Abyssinia', p. 146; Sealed Nectar, 'The First Migration to Abyssinia', p. 99
  9. c. 617
    Quraysh impose a boycott on Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, who are confined in the Shi'b of Abu Talib for about three years until the pact is annulled.7th to 10th year of prophethood · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), pp. 159, 172; Sealed Nectar, 'General Social Boycott', p. 117
  10. 619
    The Year of Grief: Abu Talib and Khadija both die. In Shawwal Muhammad ﷺ travels to al-Ta'if with Zayd ibn Haritha to invite Thaqif to Islam and is harshly rejected.10th year of prophethood · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), pp. 191-192; Sealed Nectar, 'The Year of Grief', p. 123, and 'Calling unto Islam beyond Makkah', p. 136
  11. c. 621
    The Isra' and Mi'raj: the Night Journey to Jerusalem and Ascension through the heavens. Its date is disputed; Sealed Nectar notes most place it 12-16 months before the Hijra.About 1 year before the Hijra · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'The night journey and the ascent to heaven', p. 181; Sealed Nectar, 'Al-Isra' and Al-Mi'raj', p. 147
  12. 621
    At al-Aqaba near Mina, Muslims of Yathrib give the First Pledge (621) and, the next pilgrimage season, the Second Pledge (622), promising to protect him.12th and 13th years of prophethood · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'The first pledge at al-Aqaba' and 'The second pledge at al-Aqaba', p. 201; Sealed Nectar, 'The First Aqabah Pledge', p. 154, and 'The Second Aqabah Pledge', p. 157
  13. c. 622
    The Hijra: Muhammad ﷺ and Abu Bakr migrate to Yathrib (Medina). Arrival at Quba' is 12 Rabi' al-Awwal per Ibn Ishaq, 8 Rabi' al-Awwal (23 September 622) per Sealed Nectar.1 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'Date of the hijra', p. 281; Sealed Nectar, 'Migration of the Prophet', p. 168
  14. 624
    Battle of Badr, 17 Ramadan: a small Muslim force defeats a larger Meccan army in the first decisive battle in the history of Islam.2 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'Battle of Badr', from p. 289; Sealed Nectar, 'The Battle of Badr', p. 210
  15. c. 625
    Battle of Uhud in Shawwal: the Muslims suffer heavy losses against Quraysh. The day is given as 7 Shawwal (Sealed Nectar) or mid-Shawwal (Ibn Ishaq).3 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'Battle of Uhud', from p. 370; Sealed Nectar, 'The Battle of Uhud', p. 245
  16. 627
    Battle of the Trench (al-Ahzab), Shawwal: a confederate army besieges Medina for about a month, but a defensive trench holds and the siege collapses.5 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'Battle of the Ditch' (section heading); Sealed Nectar, 'Al-Ahzab (the Confederates) Invasion', p. 311
  17. 628
    Treaty of al-Hudaybiyya in Dhu al-Qa'da establishes a truce with Quraysh. Afterwards letters inviting to Islam go to rulers including Caesar, Chosroes and the Muqawqis.6 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'The affair of al-Hudaybiya', p. 499, and 'Missions to foreign rulers', p. 652; Sealed Nectar, 'Al-Hudaibiyah Treaty', p. 339, and pp. 350-360
  18. 630
    Conquest of Mecca in Ramadan with about 10,000 men, entering with little fighting and granting safety to the inhabitants. The Battle of Hunayn follows soon after.8 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'The occupation of Mecca', p. 540, and 'Battle of Hunayn', p. 566; Sealed Nectar, 'The Conquest of Makkah', p. 388
  19. 630
    Expedition to Tabuk in Rajab against Byzantine forces, without battle. The following months, called the Year of Delegations, see tribes across Arabia send deputations.9 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'Raid on Tabuk', p. 602, and 'The Year of the Deputations', p. 627; Sealed Nectar, 'The Invasion of Tabuk', p. 422, and 'The Delegations', p. 443
  20. 632
    Farewell Pilgrimage in Dhu al-Hijja 10 AH. Muhammad ﷺ passes away in Medina on Monday 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH (June 632), aged 63 lunar years.10–11 AH · Ibn Hisham (Guillaume), 'The farewell pilgrimage', p. 649, and 'His death', p. 682; Sealed Nectar, 'The Farewell Pilgrimage', p. 461, and 'The Prophet breathes his Last', p. 478; Britannica (8 June 632)

Countries are today’s borders, used only as a way in, and imply no position on present-day disputes. Arabian dates follow Ibn Hishām (tr. Guillaume) and al-Mubārakpūrī, The Sealed Nectar.
